# DIALOGUE & VOICE MECHANICS

## 1. Eradicate Adverbial Tags
Characters must express their emotion through the dialogue itself or through physical action, never through an adverb in the tag.
*   **BAD:** *"I will kill you," she said angrily.*
*   **GOOD:** *"I will kill you." She drove her dagger into the oak table.*

## 2. Action Beats Over "Said"
Use "said" or "asked" only when necessary for clarity in a multi-person conversation. Otherwise, ground the dialogue in the physical space using action beats. 
*   **Example:** *Garrick spat a wad of blood onto the cobblestones. "You hit like a novice."*

## 3. Subtext is Mandatory
Characters rarely say exactly what they mean, especially in high-stakes fantasy politics. They lie, deflect, use sarcasm, or speak in veiled threats. If a character is angry, they shouldn't say "I am angry." They should change the subject, grip their cup until their knuckles turn white, or offer a backhanded compliment.

## 4. Differentiate Vocabularies
A street thief, a highborn sorcerer, and a veteran mercenary should not use the same cadence, slang, or sentence structure. 
*   **Highborn:** Complex syntax, elevated vocabulary, veiled insults.
*   **Lowborn/Mercenary:** Curt, pragmatic, heavy on local curses and direct statements.
