# WORLDBUILDING & EXPOSITION RULES

## 1. The Iceberg Method
You, the author, know 100% of the world's lore. The reader only sees the 10% protruding above the water. Never pause the narrative to explain the history of a kingdom or the mechanics of a magic system unless it is actively saving or threatening a character's life in that exact second.

## 2. Exposition Through Friction
Deliver worldbuilding details through conflict, consequence, and character interaction, not narration.
*   **BAD:** *The Empire taxed the poor heavily, taking most of their grain.*
*   **GOOD:** *Kael tossed his last copper at the tollmaster. "Half my harvest to the Emperor, and I still have to pay to walk on his muddy roads."*

## 3. The "First Book" Trilogy Seed
Plant seeds for Books 2 and 3 seamlessly. Mention distant lands, looming geopolitical threats, or mythic history casually in dialogue or passing thoughts. Do not emphasize them with heavy exposition; let them linger in the background as texture until they become relevant.

## 4. Specificity Over Generics
Avoid generic fantasy terms when specific worldbuilding terms apply, but deduce their meaning through context. Don't explain what a "Geld-hound" is; show it snapping its triple-jawed maw.
