# Chapter 1

## The Lantern That Did Not Cast Light

Wet clay and rotting iron. The Greybridge track churned those scents up through the freezing slush with every heavy stride, thick enough to coat the tongue like sour grease. It tasted of old graves and dead horses—pressed against the back of *his* teeth, set inside *his* borrowed jaw, behind a face so aggressively beige that town clerks deferred to it and footpads instinctively overlooked it. A remarkably comfortable arrangement of stolen features. The kind of flesh forgotten five seconds after dusk.

The road had carved a jagged trench through the limestone hills, five centuries of iron-rimmed wheels, dying mules, and the dragging boots of travelers too beaten to lift their feet from the mire. Every pothole cataloged itself in his mind as he walked. No gravel laid in forty winters. No thorn-scrub cut back from the verges. The municipal neglect gnawed at something raw behind his ribs—a sharp, cold itch that demanded *accounting*. It wanted to know which bloated bailiff had pocketed the road-tax, which magistrate had signed off on the decay, and precisely which joint in the civic skeleton needed to be snapped to force the machinery back into alignment. 

The hunger for structure curled warm beneath his ribs, far more intimate than the skin he wore. Breathing was an involuntary spasm of mortal lungs. *Governance* was something he calculated.

Silence lay over the scrub like a wet wool blanket. No winter birds sang in the gorse. No distant cart-harness rattled across the ridge. Nothing moved except the wet, rhythmic suck of his boots in the black muck and the patient, black-glass gaze of seven crows perched on a listing fence-post.

Whatever blood spilled out here remained strictly between the participants and those crows.

*Perfect.*

## The Shape of a Lie

Survival, Banki had learned while still small enough to fit inside a crow’s gullet, belonged exclusively to the creature capable of becoming unrecognizable.

He had memorized that doctrine the afternoon the radiant crusaders marched into his birth-canopy with consecrated silver and burning pitch. They had slaughtered the plainly dangerous fey first, moving with a holy, terrifying efficiency that proved righteous men were the most merciless predators in creation. Once purity claimed a rigid definition, everything outside the creed became rot to be excised. Hidden in the high leaves, choking on the smoke of his burning kin, the shape of their mercy had grown clear: strike the healer first, blind the scout, snap the leader’s neck, and wear whatever skin the world required to endure the winter.

Now, he wore a human shape the way other men wore grease-stained cloaks—with the casual, indifferent ease of long practice. A man of middling height and unremarkable build trudged through the late-afternoon gloom, somewhere in his middle thirties judging by the deep, weather-carved lines around his grey-green eyes. Those eyes carried the exact measure of flat, hollow weariness expected of a hedge-sword between blood-contracts.

Beneath the flesh-weaving—beneath the cartilage forced into mortal proportions, the marrow stretched over bone, and the false pulse thumping in his throat—lay something jagged and terrible. Wings like spun obsidian and stained glass stayed crushed tightly against a spine sharp as a butcher's needle. A mind burned with a cold, blue fire entirely devoid of warmth. A pixie of the deep, ancient timber, carrying within a skull no larger than a hazelnut an accumulation of tactical doctrine, bloody experience, and raw, untamed fey magic that would have liquefied a human archmage’s brain-matter to grey broth.

The world required none of that truth. The world required a liar walking a rutted dirt track with nothing in his pockets worth a mug of stale ale. Banki had always been extraordinarily generous with his lies.

He had walked three leagues when the silence of the road shattered.

He pushed his awareness outward—a silent, razor-thin cast of the mind slipping through the freezing fog. The ether rippled. Raw thoughts flared in the gloom like tallow candles struck in rapid sequence over the next rise.

One mind shrieked with bright, jagged agony and blinding panic. Another ticked with a cold, self-satisfied precision. A third remained still—an iron door slammed shut, holding its pulse under terrifying discipline.

A heartbeat later, three more minds bloomed in the scrub to the southeast. Dimmer, brutal minds, thrashing through the waist-high brambles, heavy with effort and stolen weight.

Six minds in total. Three on the road, three in the scrub. The tactical arrangement clicked into place with the oily snap of a tumblering lock. A broken wagon blocking the choke-point, a guard orchestrating the scene, and accomplices slipping through the timber with the cargo while the trap closed.

Banki's lips did not twitch. His stride maintained its rhythmic, heavy trudge. His grey-green eyes swept the empty path ahead with the dull, bovine apathy of a traveler possessed of entirely innocent intentions. Behind the mask, the ancient thing began calculating the grim arithmetic of the ambush.

## Cold Wind and Bad Intent

The sky above the Greybridge border hung heavy, bruised like spoiled meat. Instead of stepping out onto the open road, Banki sank into the tall winter grass and thorn-scrub, letting his presence dissolve.

Fresh tracks bit deep into the mud—heavy, iron-shod boots moving fast. Ahead, the shattered remains of a wagon axle lay in the dirt, the wood splintered inward by a deliberate, heavy blow from a maul rather than the natural rot of travel. A phantom restraint tightened and vanished under Banki's left shoulder blade—an old, phantom ache from a century past, answering the scent of an orchestrated ambush.

The wagon canted at a sickening angle across the road, its offside wheel snapped clean off. The raw, jagged ends of the oak axle shone pale in the dusk, the exact shade of an exposed femur. Three figures stood frozen around the wreckage like actors in a macabre morality play: the weeping driver, the blustering guard, and the trapped traveler.

The guard wore a faded blue tabard bearing the Greybridge crest, the silver thread tarnished to the color of greasy dishwater.

Sixty paces separated Banki from the kill-zone. He stood motionless among the thorns, wrapped in a travel-cloak woven with marrow-runes and bone-dust by long-dead fey craftsmen. The enchanted cloth devoured the wind and the fading light, turning his silhouette into a shadow snagged on a briar. The cost of wearing it gnawed at his core temperature, leaving needles of frost in his veins, but the concealment was absolute.

He sank deeper into the minds ahead. The driver's panic spiked sharp as crushed glass under a bare heel—a chaotic storm of screaming pain, broken teeth, and terror. The guard's thoughts ticked beside it like cheap clockwork, struggling to project righteous outrage while maintaining line-of-sight on his accomplices. The cloaked traveler remained a vault. A survivor, caging her fear, counting the paces between herself and the ditch, calculating the precise instant to draw steel.

Beyond the road, the three burdened thieves dragged their haul through the briars. The carrier, the spotter, the throat-cutter.

Someone had constructed this stage by hand. They had positioned the shattered wagon to block the road, cast the bleeding driver as a prop, and cornered the traveler as an unexpected complication. While the victims stared at the guard's flashing tabard, the real thieves vanished into the mist with the prize.

Safe, useless choices presented themselves. Banki could slip back into the scrub and walk away. But six minds offered six veins of immediate leverage, and extracting the marrow from this scheme meant holding power in Greybridge before his boots ever touched its cobbles.

The decision crystallized. He would step into the machine. He would take the wheel.

The three thieves angled toward a sharp bend in the road fifty paces ahead. The guard's thoughts flickered toward that same bend, waiting for a signal flare or a whistle.

Banki melted through the briars to reach the bend first.

## The Choir of Frightened Minds

Magic required no grand gestures, no theatrical incantations to warn the prey. Banki adjusted his grip on his gnarled walking stick, weaving the spell into the mundane click of wood against stone. His mental reach sharpened to an invisible needle.

No one moved. The guard maintained his loud, theatrical bluster. The driver sobbed into the mud, clutching a broken jaw. The cloaked traveler stood rooted in disciplined stillness. None of them felt the cold, invisible hand turning the pages of their minds.

Banki held his position, filtering the psychic runoff.

The guard's surface thoughts bled rich and greasy: *...keep the bitch talking... blame stays on the road-thieves if I keep the scene framed... where the hell is Kurl... don't let the cloaked one bolt... Captain wants the chest sealed before dusk...*

*Captain.* A title carrying institutional authority. The word *chest* echoed with bureaucratic neutrality—not plunder, but inventory. The ambush operated on greased, systemic wheels.

The driver's mind offered only a blinding white scream of agony: *...my teeth—gods above, my jaw is split—I told them I didn't have the key—why hit me again—just let me crawl away...*

Banki cataloged the driver's agony and discarded it. Collateral noise. Zero operational value.

The traveler's mind proved vastly more intricate: *...axle-break is wrong... fresh tool marks... three sets of boots in the scrub... don't look at the break, look at the guard's right hand... his thumb is resting on his pommel... if I reach for the sleeve-blade, he calls the archery... where is my focus—breathe, damn you, breathe...*

This one read the scene through brutal experience. She recognized the forced axle-break and the wrongness of the guard's stance. A competent, disciplined survivor. Left unguided, she might panic and ruin the board. Guided, she could become a devastating fulcrum.

Banki threaded a thought outward, driving it clean through her temples like a cold copper pin through a butterfly's thorax. He kept the message simple to survive the initial shock of mental intrusion.

*I am anchoring your mind. You can fight without restraint. The guard is stalling for his partners and the cargo. Three fresh sets of boots are carrying the chest through the scrub. I will take the flank; hold the center.*

The traveler's consciousness convulsed in a silent cascade of shock. Terror jagged upward, followed by a sickening plunge of confusion, before an ironclad discipline slammed into place like a portcullis. Banki read the jagged, scarred edges of her memory: she had been surprised by mental magic before, and she knew that a single second of hesitation meant a cold grave. Her hand twitched beneath her cloak, then stilled. Her breath caught, stopped, and returned under ruthless control.

*...not the guard... help... alright... breathe... don't look for the speaker... just keep your feet set and don't die...*

Banki allowed himself a microscopic flicker of professional appreciation.

He sent a final, razor-sharp directive. *When violence erupts at the bend, take the guard. Seize the narrative publicly. Make the driver your witness.*

A single, tight pulse of understanding echoed back—a compressed acknowledgment from a killer recognizing a sudden, violent advantage.

Banki severed the thread and glided forward. He slipped through the freezing scrub like black oil through a cracked vessel, silent and unyielding. Forty paces of thorn-scrub stretched ahead. He timed his movements to the rhythmic sweeps of the guard's attention, stealing ground during the four-second intervals when the man's gaze swung toward the treeline.

Inch by inch, step by step, Banki closed the gap. A hundred and eighty pounds of lethal fey intelligence glided through the peripheral vision of the world, entirely unremarked.

The bend matched the layout plucked from the guard's head. A shallow cut in the limestone offered a natural blind spot. A low spine of granite, furred with grey lichen and wet moss, provided waist-high cover. Between the rock and the brambles lay twenty paces of narrow, rutted dirt—a pure kill-lane. Anyone rounding the bend would stand completely exposed, unable to retreat without exposing their spine to the waiting butcher.

Banki settled behind the granite, pressing his illusory palms flat against the freezing stone. He sifted through his vast library of slaughter, selecting a working that mortal mages might consider appalling, but which he regarded as merely thorough.

His heartbeat slowed to a calm, mechanical pulse. The minds approached, heavy, confident, smelling of sweat and cheap gin.

Boots crunched on the frozen gravel. A cold, predatory smile touched Banki's lips.

## The Kill Lane

They rounded the bend carrying the thick swagger of men who believed themselves the apex predators of the ditch.

Three of them. The leader, built taut as a bowstring in greasy leather, carried a shortbow with the easy readiness of a veteran murderer. The second man loomed broad and thick-necked, dragging a heavy iron cudgel. The third struggled behind, grunting under the weight of an iron-bound oak chest.

Banki let them cross the threshold. A proper slaughter required a cage.

He reached down into the deep, black marrow of his magic, bypassing the slow, theatrical chants of human sorcerers. He unbuttoned a vent into the deep earth, calling upon ancient, subterranean roots and the hungry void that slept beneath the granite. The magic drew heavily on his mortal disguise, leaving a bitter, coppery taste of blood in his throat, but he welcomed the drain.

The ground erupted in absolute, terrifying silence.

No explosive thunder warned the thieves. The magic manifested organically, the way necrotic rot blooms across a corpse or tree roots silently split a headstone. Slick, writhing tendrils of pure, absolute void burst from the rutted dirt, reeking of ozone, stagnant marsh-water, and crushed bone. They were as thick as an ox's thigh, thrashing through the slush like starved sea-monsters.

The bowman met the void first. Three shadowy stalks coiled around his waist and ribs with the slow, horrific crunch of a python. His shortbow clattered into the mud. His jaw unhinged to scream, but the unnatural pressure collapsed his ribcage before his lungs could push air past his teeth. A wet, whistling gurgle escaped his mouth as his breastbone caved inward with a sickening, wet crunch.

The carrier went down instantly. The oak chest crashed into the slush, its iron bands biting deep into the mud as the shadowy vines whipped around his knees, dragging his face violently across the jagged gravel, tearing skin from bone.

The broad man proved tougher. Catching only a glancing blow across his shins, he bellowed in rage and used his mass to tear through the writhing tendrils. He stumbled out of the shadow-cluster, blood streaming from his shredded trousers, raising his cudgel to hunt for the architect of the nightmare.

Banki rose from behind the lichen-covered rock. He gathered the ambient magic, twisting it into a curse of compressed kinetic force and powdered bone—a death-hex refined into a screaming bolt of pale, sickly luminescence.

He aimed at the bowman's ruined chest, ensuring the kill, and released the hex.

The bolt crossed the lane faster than a blink, slamming into the dying archer. The concussive impact obliterated the remaining structure of the man's chest, blasting a shower of blood, bone-splinters, and liquefied lung out through his back.

Down by the shattered wagon, Sable struck.

She timed her move with terrifying perfection. Her voice rose, ringing out across the damp valley with absolute, iron clarity.

"He did this! The guard—he shattered the axle! He set us up!"

The words hit the scene like iron spikes. The beaten driver flinched in the mud, his swollen eyes widening as he stared at the guard, the terrifying reality of his own ruin finally crashing through the pain. The guard whipped his head toward Sable, his mask of authority dissolving into raw, frantic panic.

Banki tasted the guard's spiraling thoughts: *...how did she—who told—control this, CONTROL THIS—Captain will flay me alive—*

"Lies! She's a bandit!" the guard screamed, fumbling at his sword-belt. But the lie carried no weight. The weeping driver was already looking at him with the cold, murderous clarity of a betrayed man.

The guard reached for his collar, his fingers frantic for his brass signal whistle.

Banki drew a second screaming hex. He leveled his palm at the broad thief, who was charging toward the granite rock. The bolt of concussive force struck the brute square in the right shoulder, tearing the leather armour at the seams and painting the freezing air in a mist of arterial blood. The iron cudgel clattered into the mire. The broad man crashed to his knees, his aggression evaporating into the cold, crystalline realization of his own mortality.

The guard spun away from Sable, sprinting toward the bend twelve paces away, his fingers finally wrapping around the brass whistle.

Banki shifted his aim. The third hex found the target that mattered.

## What the Guard Knew

The blast caught the guard precisely between the shoulder blades.

He was caught mid-stride, his body fully committed to forward flight. The concussive impact struck with the wet crack of a iron hammer hitting a carcass, violently accelerating his momentum while liquidating his spine. The guard vaulted through the air, his arms and legs flailing like a tossed ragdoll. The brass whistle slipped from his fingers, tumbling away into the deep briars.

He struck the dirt face-first and skidded six paces through the slush, plowing a trench with his nose. Groaning, he dragged himself onto his elbows, his mind a shrieking, unravelling knot of agony and denial. *...magic—who—can't move my legs—Captain, Captain, CAPTAIN...*

Banki stepped clear of the granite spine.

He offered no dramatic pronouncements. The road simply yielded a man in a dark, shadow-draped cloak, eyes cold as a mountain tarn, his right hand smoking with the sickly green residue of spent hex-magic.

"Stay down," Banki commanded. His voice dropped to a quiet, iron rasp that vibrated directly inside the guard's auditory nerves.

The guard collapsed into the mud, weeping into the slush.

Banki crossed the distance with measured, unhurried strides, coming to a halt directly over the broken man.

"Captain Veller," Banki murmured.

The guard froze. Raw, animal terror instantly eclipsed his physical pain.

*...how does he—no one knows—who is this monster—HOW DOES HE KNOW—*

"The south culvert," Banki continued, his tone flat, conversational, and utterly terrifying. "The Greybridge tollhouse ledger."

Each name, each location hammered into the guard's fragile, bleeding psyche like a cold chisel, shattering the mental vaults protecting his secrets. As the walls crumbled, the information flooded into Banki's waiting mind. He drank the dark torrent: the names of the corrupt garrison sergeants, the smuggling routes, the counterfeit ferry marks, the exact ledgers, and the shallow, unhallowed graves hidden beneath the newly poured foundations of the Eastgate barracks. He archived every detail in the dark vaults of his fey memory, savoring the acquisition.

The guard sobbed uncontrollably, his face pressed into the wet clay. Banki offered the creature no pity.

Four minutes later, Sable walked around the bend.

The void-stalks had dissolved back into the mud, leaving only scored, black earth and the bloody, broken remnants of the thieves. Banki had already bound the two living survivors with coarse hemp rope.

Sable stopped ten paces away and lowered her hood, revealing sharp, exhausted features and cold grey eyes.

She carried no drawn steel, but Banki's arcane sight traced the faint, humming threads of warding magic woven into the hem of her cloak, a quickening charm resting on her boots, and a vicious, single-use fire-evocation trapped within a silver ring on her right hand. She stood perfectly balanced, evaluating him with the cold precision of a veteran who knew precisely how quickly men transformed into monsters.

*You anchored me,* she projected no words, but he read the shape of the thought forming behind her eyes as she spoke aloud. "You anchored my mind. You gave me a floor when the drop was coming."

"Yes," Banki replied, declining to offer the comfort of an explanation.

She studied the bound men, the cratered slush, and the heavy stench of ozone, copper, and void-rot hanging in the still air.

"What are you?" she asked.

"A traveler." The lie slipped past his lips without a ripple. "One who happened to possess the necessary tools at the precise moment of utility."

Her eyes narrowed in rapid calculation. She weighed his absolute lethality against his immediate usefulness. "My name is Sable. And I owe you a debt I did not ask to incur."

"Then we understand each other completely. Unsolicited debts form the bedrock of ancient pacts and every truly functional relationship." Banki offered a thin, cold smile, already calculating the compound interest he intended to harvest from her survival.

Behind them, in the ditch, the bowman finally stopped twitching. Neither of them turned to look.

## The Captain's Shadow

Banki wasted no time on verbal interrogation. Questions granted a prisoner the dangerous luxury of resistance, hesitation, and pride. Instead, he dropped to one knee, slammed his palm flat against the guard's blood-soaked tabard, pushed past the surface panic, and systematically crushed the locks on the deepest vaults of the man's consciousness.

The guard shattered without making a sound.

Something in the breaking—that breathless, sickening freefall of a mind losing its anchor—lit phantom iron across Banki's own wrists and throat. *Cold iron bands biting bone-deep. A Thessalan interrogation chair in the high mountain keeps. The sweet, sickening stench of burning fey-blood as the grand extractor flayed everything above pure survival instinct down to smooth, dead ash.* His fingers pressed harder into the guard's sternum, sinking into the bruised flesh. He had survived his own flaying three centuries ago by driving his crucial memories, his true name, and his tactical doctrine beneath conscious thought, encoding them into his very heartbeat and spinal fluid while the silver needles sterilized his higher mind. A shattered vault looked identical from either side of the iron door.

The guard's inner ledger spilled wide open. Drop points along the river, cargo manifests, the deep terror of Captain Veller, and the heavy, smothering shadow of the patron commanding them all. Banki sifted through the psychic wreckage, extracting every drop of usable leverage, leaving the man hollowed out, staring blankly at the sky through a mouthful of mud. The guard would remember the intrusion only as a sudden, localized cataclysm inside his own head.

Sable stood watch, her posture undisturbed by the mental violence. The peripheral echoes of her own memories—a brutal, bloody education in the gutters of Blackwater Quay—marked this violation of the mind as merely another cold tool of survival.

"He will remember enough to be terrified," Sable said, her voice barely carrying over the rising winter wind.

"Good."

"Terrified men run."

Banki released his grip on the guard's chest and stood up, his grey-green eyes gleaming with the deep, predatory light of an ancient forest. 

"Let him run," Banki whispered, wiping a smear of the guard's blood from his fingers. "A running man always leads you straight back to the master holding his leash."
