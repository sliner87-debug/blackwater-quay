# Chapter 20: The Abyssal Trench & The Primary Engine

The water was no longer water. It had long since surrendered its fluid nature, somewhere past the suffocating twilight zone where the sun’s last sickly, anaemic rays were strangled into absolute black. Down here, in the abyssal trench, the ocean was a colossal, living vice—a titan’s fist forged from black ice, necrotic salt, and sheer, pulverizing pressure. It pressed inward with the cold, unyielding mathematics of annihilation, ravenous to pulp muscle, snap longbones like brittle dry pine, and crush a sovereign human form into a crimson smear of pressurized carbon.

One thousand fathoms. Then two. The descent was not a mere passage through the dark; it was a slow, calculated act of blasphemous defiance against a hostile world. The abyss offered no welcome. It only hungered.

Arcane static whipped across the Tri-Weave, violent tendrils of violet arcana and jagged, arterial white tearing through the pitch-black void. Deep within the weave, the sovereign threads hummed—a high-pitched, agonizing vibration that sang directly against the base of the skull, bypassing the ears entirely. That hum was Sable. Miles above, braced on the pitching, blood-slick deck of the dreadnought, her boots locked into cold iron, she held the primary anchor of the Sovereign Thread. The tether was a metaphysical lifeline, a taut cord of mutual survival and transactional necessity dropped straight into the crushing deep. She bore the brutal, bone-splitting arithmetic of the anchor, taking the psychic recoil into her own marrow so the violence below could be executed without hesitation.

In the right hand, Null-Fang trembled. Its sovereign metal groaned against the staggering geological weight bearing down upon the wards. The blade drank the ambient darkness, rendering the surrounding brine even blacker—a black hole suspended within an infinite void.

Pressure in the abyss was far more than physical force; it was a psychological hammer. It compressed the ribcage until the lungs felt flattened to thin parchment, crushed the lumbar spine until cartilage ground against bone, and drove the eyeballs painfully backward into their sockets. Every draft drawn from the internalized, arcane pocket of recycled air tasted of scorched copper, stale ozone, and the sour, metallic tang of adrenaline.

The cultists of the Drowned Choir had preached salvation in the dark. They sang of a divine resonance, a holy, oceanic communion waiting in the deep to cradle the broken in eternal peace. The reality was a cathedral of wet horrors. Down here, there was no god—only cold, ruthless arithmetic. The arithmetic of flesh fighting to exist where reality demanded a singularity.

The wards flared white-hot. Psionic shielding, tightly interlocked with warlock invocations, spun a fragile, translucent sphere of normalized reality against the abyss. The ablative layers of the Cerebrilith Mantle stripped away in blistering sheets under the immense strain, instantly re-knitted by conscious, agonizing effort. It was a relentless cycle of destruction and reconstruction, demanding absolute, white-knuckle concentration merely to preserve a human volume in the deep.

Down. Always down.

The abyssal current shifted. It did not flow; it dragged, viscous and thick as cooling blood. Long, rotting strands of coagulated marine snow drifted past the edge of the ward-bubble, glowing with a sickly, bioluminescent rot. The pale flakes stuck to the outer skin of the psionic shield, hissing like lye as they dissolved against the raw telekinetic force. The cold seeped through the defensive layers regardless—an insidious, glacial numbness that crawled up the forearms, settled into the joints, and turned the blood into thick, sluggish sludge.

Then a shadow stirred in the absolute dark. It was a shifting disturbance in the blackness, a shape far too massive to be a natural beast, far too aggressively angular to be a formation of stone.

Marker Nineteen.

It jutted from the floor of the trench like a rotting, iron tooth. An obelisk of corroded infernal iron and calcified coral, completely smothered in blind, pale anemones that throbbed in a sickening, arrhythmic pulse. The marker was no beacon of hope. It was a gravitational tether—an anchor dropped into a sea of sensory death.

The descent slowed. Telekinetic thrusters flared from the soles of the boots, kicking up a sudden, violent storm of fine, bone-white silt from the trench floor. The boots touched down. No sound carried through the crushing water; instead, a deep, resonant thud slammed up through the soles, vibrating through the shins and rattling the teeth within the jaw.

Gravity reasserted its grip—heavy, sluggish, and profoundly unnatural. The wards adjusted with a shrill, metallic whine that sounded like overstressed iron gears tearing themselves apart.

The obelisk towered into the black, bleeding a faint, discordant hum into the brine. It vibrated at a frequency that set the nerves on edge, a low, thrumming pulse that bypassed the eardrums and drilled directly into the jawbone. It tasted of spoiled milk and rusted iron on the back of the tongue.

The Drowned Choir’s sacred hymns were nothing more than distorted, diluted echoes of this mechanical noise. Up on the surface, among the rain-slick alleys and flooded cellars of Greyquay, the desperate called it the voice of a god. Down here, standing at the roots of the world, the truth was stark and filthy: it was merely the friction of a broken, infernal engine grinding through the bedrock.

As the silt storm slowly settled, the true floor of the trench was laid bare. It was not rock. It was not sand. It was an undulating, pale carpet of bones.

Leviathan ribs, arching like the ruined, shattered vaulting of a drowned cathedral, formed pale cages in the dark. Skulls of prehistoric leviathans stared with wide, hollow sockets filled with pale, blind worms that writhed in the sluggish current. And scattered among those gargantuan remains lay smaller skeletons. Humanoid bones. Thousands of them. The faithful of the Drowned Choir, cast into the sea to meet their god, finding only the crushing dark and the ravenous horrors that swam within it.

The edge of the ward-bubble scraped against a massive, fossilized femur. The friction released a sudden, brilliant shower of violet sparks into the water, illuminating the grotesque ossuary in blinding, flickering flashes.

The Tri-Weave flared violently. Not from the pressure. Not from the cold.

A psychic burr, rough and heavily barbed, dragged across the outer threshold of the mind. It was not the mechanical hum of the Marker. It was a living, breathing malice—hungry, ancient, and acutely aware.

The water temperature plummeted past freezing, kept liquid only by the extreme pressure. Frost flowered across the inside of the ward-bubble, intricate crystalline patterns mapping the microscopic stress fractures of the shield.

A sudden pulse in the dark. A displacement of water so massive and fast it hit like a physical wave.

The Cerebrilith.

It did not swim. It projected its physical mass through the abyss, riding a wave of localized telekinetic force.

The absolute blackness split open. A nightmare of exposed, undulating gray matter and pulsing, bioluminescent violet veins tore into the faint light of the wards. It was colossal—a floating, grotesque parody of a human brain encased in a translucent, heavily armored chitinous shell. Dozens of barbed, prehensile tentacles trailed beneath its main bulk, whipping through the water, tasting the currents, hunting for a sovereign mind to crack open and devour.

Its central mass throbbed with an inner, malignant light that cast long, distorted shadows across the bone-field. The psionic gravity of the creature was staggering; it was not merely an apex predator, but a localized tear in reality—a psychic black hole dragging every thought toward its ravenous center.

The barbed burr transformed into a jagged iron spike, hammering against the mind’s defenses with the force of a siege engine. A screech of pure, unadulterated malice tore through the skull, demanding instant, total submission.

The battle would not be physical. The water was too dense, the pressure too absolute for conventional steel. This was a slaughter of minds—a grinding, brutal war of attrition between sovereign will and aberrant hunger.

The Cerebrilith lunged forward, the deep parting violently around its bulk. Tentacles lashed out, striking the outer skin of the ward-bubble with concussive violence. The shield flared a blinding, brilliant white, the Tri-Weave screaming under the impact.

The tentacles did not attempt to crush the sphere through crude physical force. They were far more insidious. Pale, fleshy suckers extruded microscopic bone-spikes, seeking purchase on the arcane structure of the shield. They were injecting a viral, psychic poison directly into the spell-matrix, designed to unweave the warding from the inside out and expose the fragile human brain within.

Null-Fang whipped upward, its void-metal humming a brutal counter-frequency. A single fluid arc severed two of the thickest appendages. The void-blade met no resistance, shearing through dense, rubbery muscle and cartilage as though passing through smoke. Black, necrotic fluid gushed into the brine.

The creature did not recoil. It possessed no nervous system capable of registering pain in the human sense; it registered only the sudden loss of data-gathering limbs. It calculated. It adjusted.

The throb of its central brain accelerated into a violent, blinding pulse. A wave of pure kinetic force erupted from its mass—a psychic shockwave so dense it visibly bent the water and light around the shield. It slammed into the bubble.

The impact was a brutal, physical blow. Knees buckled, driving iron boots deep into the bone-silt. Blood erupted from the nostrils, a sudden, hot gush that tasted sharply of copper against the throat. The Tri-Weave shrieked as interwoven strands of arcane and divine power snapped under the localized stress.

High above, the sovereign tether yanked hard against the base of the skull. A spike of absolute, unyielding focus shot downward through miles of black ocean—Sable, stabilizing the anchor, bleeding the kinetic shock straight into the dreadnought’s iron hull. She sent no panic, no frantic telepathic query. Only a cold, ruthless recalibration of the load. The synchronization held, flawless and freezing.

Focus narrowed to a single, bloody point. Peripheral vision withered into shadow until the world consisted only of the monster, its pulsating brain-mass, and the sickening violet glare illuminating the abyss.

The objective burned in the mind's eye: Cerebrilith spinal fluid. This was no mere fight for survival; it was a harvest. The Weaver demanded the fluid pristine, chemically uncorrupted, and potent. Pulverizing the creature with raw Hellfire or a crushing telekinetic field would incinerate the prize. It required a surgical execution in the darkest pit of the world.

Eldritch energy began to pool in the left hand. Not a wide, sweeping cone, but a compressed, humming lance of white-purple-black force. It vibrated at a precise frequency designed to shatter psionic shielding without scorching the biological treasure within.

The Cerebrilith struck again. Abandoning brute force, it launched a direct, surgical mind-stab—a sliver of concentrated malice slipping past the outer physical wards to sink into the architecture of the brain.

Memories twisted instantly, weaponized by the aberration. The pale faces of the fallen lying in Greyquay mud. The suffocating, lung-searing ash of the White Forge. The crushing weight of the sovereign crown pressing into the scalp. Krazak’s mocking, raspy laugh echoing in the furnace. The wet tear of flesh from bone. Every failure, every doubt, every hidden micro-fracture in the psyche was magnified, distorted, and dragged into a looping nightmare.

A whisper slid through the brain, slick and cold as glacial ice: *Surrender. The surface is fire and endless burden. The deep is quiet. The deep is peace. Lay down the crown. Lay down the blade.*

The intrusion hit a wall of absolute, unyielding sovereign iron. The mind was not built on hope or fragile peace; it had been forged in blood, iron, and the cold logic of survival. The whisper was isolated, surrounded by a cage of psionic static, quarantined from the core consciousness, and crushed into nothingness.

The counter-strike was instantaneous and unmerciful. The compressed lance of eldritch force launched from the left gauntlet.

It was the *Screaming Skull*.

Not a simple beam of power, but a concentrated manifestation of predatory will. A skull of white-purple-black force blossomed in the deep, its jaw distended in a silent scream that vibrated against the teeth, the wards, and the very nerves of the beast. It ripped through the dense water, bypassing thrashing tentacles, and struck the Cerebrilith’s chitinous shell dead center.

Where the skull hit, reality buckled. It did not burn; it bit. It cracked. It erased.

The thick, armored shell resisted for a fraction of a second, glowing with a desperate defensive flare, then shattered into a hundred fragments. Shards of chitin spiraled into the dark.

The creature convulsed in a violent, uncoordinated spasm. Tentacles whipped aimlessly, tying themselves into grotesque knots as its central intelligence was severed from its motor functions. The bioluminescent violet light flickered wildly, stuttering like a dying lamp.

Three seconds. That was the window.

Telekinetic thrusters erupted from the boots at maximum output, blowing a massive crater into the bone-field. The distance closed in a heartbeat. The ward-bubble slammed into the creature's fractured psychic field—a violent collision of opposing realities that sent shockwaves through the trench.

Null-Fang drove home. The void-metal slid precisely between the two pulsating, exposed lobes of the brain, burying itself deep in the soft, spongy gray matter. With a sharp, practiced twist, the blade severed the primary synaptic trunk.

The violet light died instantly.

The massive psionic weight pressing upon the trench evaporated, leaving a sudden, dizzying vacuum—a silence so profound it rang in the ears like a deafening bell.

The dead aberration drifted downward, a ruined mass of gray tissue and limp tentacles sinking onto the carpet of bones.

From a warded belt pouch, a specialized extraction syringe—forged by the Weaver from hardened alchemical glass and cold-forged sovereign iron—snapped into the left hand. The thick, hollow needle pierced the severed synaptic trunk, sliding straight into the fluid reservoir at the base of the brain. The plunger drew back, pulling a thick, highly viscous, luminescent blue fluid into the glass barrel.

Cerebrilith spinal fluid. Ice-cold, heavy in the palm, humming with a residual psychic pulse that made the fingertips throb.

The glass vial sealed with a sharp metallic click and was tucked safely back into the warded pouch, insulated against the crushing depths.

Harvest complete. One objective secured.

The water around Marker Nineteen settled once more, the turbulent currents generated by the fight fading into the sluggish drift of the abyss. The low, discordant hum of the obelisk resumed its dominance, grinding relentlessly against the jaw.

Beyond the Marker, the trench floor vanished entirely. The rock sheared off into a vertical cliff that dropped into a bottomless, black canyon. The source of the hum—the epicenter of the vibration—lay down there in the ultimate dark.

The Elder Node. The heart of the Drowned Choir's theology. The god they sacrificed thousands to appease.

The descent resumed. The bone-silt settled over the dead aberration, burying it in pale dust.

The canyon walls were sheer, polished mirror-smooth by impossible currents and millennia of pressure. They narrowed rapidly, funneling the descent into a tight, suffocating throat of black stone. The pressure mounted, the wards complaining with a high, sustained shriek.

With every fathom, the hum grew louder. It ceased to be sound and became a concussive physical force—a relentless, rhythmic hammer slamming against the ribs, trying to drive the breath from the lungs. The Tri-Weave strained, dynamically shifting its frequencies, harmonizing the wards to deflect the acoustic assault before it could liquefy internal organs into red soup.

Then a glow appeared below. Not the sickly bioluminescence of abyssal life, nor the violet flare of a psionic predator.

It was a necrotic, industrial green. Mechanical. Rhythmic. Pulsing.

The floor of the canyon rushed up. Thrusters fired in sharp, controlled bursts to break the descent.

The Elder Node was not a living thing. The Drowned Choir's faith was a lie.

## The Primary Engine & The Desecration of Faith

The cultists in Greyquay spoke of a slumbering leviathan. They whispered in dark cellars of an ancient, divine entity dreaming in the deep, whose sacred hymns guided the faithful to salvation and whose immense mind cradled the ocean. They worshiped a god of the abyss.

The reality at the bottom of the canyon was a sickening desecration of that belief.

It was not a god. It was not alive. It had never been alive.

It was a machine.

A colossal, necrotic engine sprawled across the canyon floor, dominating the claustrophobic space. It was half-buried in centuries of calcified bone, rusted iron slag, and thick, black necrotic sludge. The scale was staggering—a nightmare of infernal engineering defying the crushing pressure through brute force. Cyclopean gears, some as large as the town square in Sablehook and encrusted with blind barnacles, ground against each other with a screech that vibrated through the marrow. Pistons the size of siege towers pumped sluggishly, venting massive clouds of glowing green, highly toxic coolant into the poisoned water.

The Primary Engine.

A monument to ancient, abandoned infernal infrastructure, yet it refused to die. It drew raw power directly from geothermal vents anchored beneath its foundation, channeling the earth's molten blood through rusted iron pipes and cracked conduits.

At the center of the structure stood a massive containment vessel constructed of thick, reinforced glass and bands of blackened iron, blazing with that sickening green light. It was the source of the mechanical hum—the heart of the machine.

Inside the glowing cylinder, silhouettes floated in the thick, viscous coolant. Humanoid shapes.

Thirteen of them.

The thirteen victims. The ones the Drowned Choir claimed had been "restored." The ones who had achieved ultimate communion with their god.

They were not ascended beings. They were biological batteries.

Their bodies were hideously stretched and warped by the pressure and necrotic fluid. Heavy, rusted cables and thick, pulsing tubes were violently fused directly into their spines, the bases of their skulls, and their chests. Suspended in perpetual, agonizing stasis, their souls were being systematically drained to keep the rusted gears turning. Their eyes were locked wide open, glowing with the same industrial green light, their mouths frozen in silent screams that bubbled uselessly into the fluid.

The sacred hymns of the Drowned Choir—the haunting melodies that echoed through the flooded tunnels of Greyquay—were nothing more than the acoustic exhaust of a failing engine. The divine resonance was merely a mechanical feedback loop broadcasting localized psychic static, which desperate cultists above interpreted as the voice of a god.

It was a monumental, systemic lie.

The Choir had not found a deity in the deep; they had stumbled upon a forgotten, leaking machine and built a religion around its radioactive rot. They had fed their own kin into a meat grinder, believing it to be an altar, only to fuel a rusted engine.

The machine shuddered violently. A massive gear slipped its track for a fraction of a second, grinding against the outer housing with a sound that threatened to shatter the ward-bubble. The concussive wave slammed into the shield, dropping the internal temperature and threatening to crack the Tri-Weave.

This was the source of the rot infecting the coast. This was the entity luring the faithful into the ocean to transform them into hollow husks.

The mission parameters shifted instantly. Extraction of the thirteen victims was impossible; telemetry from the Tri-Weave confirmed their nervous systems were permanently fused with the necrotic circuitry, their souls tied directly to the power cycle. Unplugging them would not save them; it would tear them apart in agonizing agony.

There was no salvation for them. The only mercy left was absolute destruction.

Null-Fang could not sever a machine of this scale. Void-metal cut magic and flesh, not hundreds of tons of infernal iron. Standard eldritch blasts would merely pit the heavy armor, destroying minor conduits while the core repaired itself using geothermal power and biological fuel.

The engine had to be broken from within. Its own power had to be turned against its guts.

Telekinetic thrusters engaged, driving the body forward through a maze of grinding teeth, snapping cables, and boiling coolant plumes. Heat radiating from the engine core was intense, creating pockets of superheated water that battered the wards and threatened to boil the air inside the sphere.

The primary control console sat at the base of the containment cylinder, shadowed by the glowing tank above. A massive panel of rusted levers, complex brass dials, and deep, arcane input ports designed for hands far larger than human. Infernal hands.

The approach triggered automated defenses. As the ward-bubble crossed a rusted threshold, heavy iron plates on the canyon floor slid back.

Automated sentry-nodes—crusted in sharp barnacles and pale tube-worms—rose from hidden pits. Cracked, glowing optics swiveled toward the intruder. They did not fire bullets; they launched lances of concentrated geothermal heat, aiming to boil the trespasser alive inside their own wards.

Three beams of blinding white heat struck the Tri-Weave at once. The wards flared an agonizing blue, the arcane threads screaming under the thermal load. The temperature inside the bubble spiked, sweat instantly beading on the forehead and evaporating into the dry air.

Eldritch energy answered. The right hand, gripping Null-Fang, channeled the power. Three rapid *Screaming Skull* blasts erupted from the gauntlet's knuckles.

The white-purple-black skulls ripped through the boiling water. They did not merely hit the sentry-nodes; they bit into their casings, shattering the armored shells to erase the arcane logic-cores within. The nodes detonated in muffled, concussive thuds, reduced to glowing molten slag that rapidly hardened against the cold seabed.

The path to the console was clear.

Boots landed on the rusted iron grating of the control platform. The vibration here was punishing—a constant, bone-rattling shake that blurred vision. Directly above, the thirteen victims drifted in their glowing green prison, their silent screams illuminated by the necrotic glare.

The input ports on the console were heavily warded behind overlapping infernal ciphers, built to unleash lethal magical feedback upon any unauthorized touch.

There was no time for delicate unraveling. No time for ciphers.

The left hand—encased in the armored gauntlet of the Sovereign Ascendancy—slammed down onto the primary interface plate, the metal groaning under the impact.

A forced connection. A violent, non-consensual integration of sovereign will into a hostile, necrotic system.

The instant the gauntlet made contact, the mind was flooded with raw, chaotic data—failing systems, cracked conduits, wild power surges, and, above all, the agonizing, collective shriek of the thirteen souls trapped in the vessel. Their pain was not abstract; it poured through the link as raw, unfiltered sensation—a deafening scream that filled the skull from the inside.

The volume of data was a physical force, a psychic storm attempting to tear consciousness from flesh. Vision went gray, replaced by cascading walls of infernal script and chaotic diagnostic streams.

Through the deafening noise, a cold, mechanical voice echoed: *Submit. Integrate. Power the cycle. The engine requires fuel. The engine must turn. Join the Choir.*

The response was not a rejection. It was a localized, devastating application of sovereign will—a psychic wedge forged from the cold authority of Sablehook and the total denial of subjugation, driven straight into the engine's command core.

The Tri-Weave bypassed defense protocols. Arcane, divine, and psionic threads were braided together, channeled down the arm, through the gauntlet, and into the rusted metal. The sovereign force violently overrode the infernal ciphers, brute-forcing the mechanical locks with rapidly escalating kinetic shocks. The console's wards shattered one by one, unable to withstand a command structure built on pure, unyielding dominance.

The goal was not a safe shutdown. A safe shutdown would allow the engine to self-repair. The goal was catastrophic overload—forcing the core past the physical limits of the containment vessel.

Mental commands, translated into raw arcane directives, flooded the system. Power was forcefully diverted from the main coolant pumps. The intake valves slammed shut.

Instantly, the engine's rhythm broke. The massive pistons groaned, their steady thud faltering as the internal core temperature spiked wildly. The green light radiating from the cylinder intensified, shifting from a dull glow to a blinding, agonizing glare that pierced the abyssal dark. Water around the core began to boil violently, churning into clouds of superheated steam.

The engine fought back. Deep in its rusted guts, emergency bypass valves attempted to force themselves open—ancient protocols fighting for survival. Dormant coolant lines shuddered, trying to flood the core.

Every emergency protocol was crushed.

Every attempt to restore balance was met with a brutal counter-command. A localized kinetic strike to a valve assembly. A psionic short-circuit across a pump array. A raw imposition of will forcing the engine deeper into the red, denying every avenue of thermal release.

Vibrations tearing through the console grew terrifying, threatening to rip the arm from its socket. The canyon walls shuddered violently as seismic shocks dislodged massive slabs of black rock. Boulders the size of houses plunged through the dark, smashing into exposed gears and shattering rusted steam pipes.

The heat radiating from the core became unbearable, burning through the layers of the ward-bubble. Air inside the sphere grew scorching hot, blistering skin across the face and hands. The Tri-Weave whined—a high, continuous pitch of impending structural collapse. The bubble deformed as boiling seawater pressed inward with crushing force.

The machine screamed. Not with a voice, but with heat, pressure, and the shriek of metal forced far past its breaking point. The containment vessel turned blinding white-green, the thick glass webbed with fractures that spread faster than thought.

The override was sealed. The destruction sequence was locked into the matrix beyond any automated recovery.

The hand tore away from the console. The connection snapped with a concussive discharge of arcane static that threw the body backward off the platform.

Telekinetic thrusters erupted at maximum, dangerous output. The boots unleashed raw force, launching the body upward—away from the boiling, shuddering engine, away from the screaming glass cylinder, away from the Elder Node.

The ascent was no controlled climb; it was a violent, desperate rocket launch toward the surface.

Below, the Primary Engine finally tore itself to pieces.

There was no sound—only a massive, silent expansion of absolute ruin in the dark. A shockwave of superheated water, raw necrotic energy, and thousands of tons of shattered infernal iron expanded outward in a flawless sphere of destruction.

The central containment vessel shattered. A blinding flash of pure, searing green light illuminated the entire canyon for a fraction of a second, stripping every shadow bare. The blast instantly vaporized the physical forms of the thirteen victims, ejecting their trapped soul-essence into the crushing deep.

The shockwave slammed into the ascending ward-bubble like a solid iron wall.

The impact launched the sphere upward at terrifying speed. The wards held by a thread, the Tri-Weave glowing white-hot under the stress. Through the chaos, the sovereign tether yanked hard, reeling the asset in. Sable was hauling the line taut miles above—a ruthless, mechanized retrieval, pulling the prize from the inferno with the cold efficiency of an apex predator recovering her own.

Looking down through the churning water, the canyon was a boiling cauldron of steam, iron fragments, and fading green radiation. The massive gears were pulverised, the conduits torn away, the containment vessel erased.

The god of the Drowned Choir was dead.

The sacred hymns echoing through Greyquay would fall silent. The divine resonance was dead. The horrific religion built around a rusted, leaking machine was smashed to dust.

No more sacrifices to the deep. No more souls wired into necrotic metal.

Only the cold, dark ocean remained, rushing in to fill the void, burying the shattered bones of the Primary Engine under fresh silt and silence.
