# Blackwater Quay — The Complete Omnibus

## The Lantern That Did Not Cast Light

Wet dirt and old iron. The Greybridge road pressed those scents up through the mud with every step, thick enough to taste against the back of the teeth—*his* teeth, set into *his* jaw, behind a face that clerks deferred to and liars instinctively overlooked. A useful arrangement of borrowed features. The kind forgotten by dusk.

The track had carved itself deep into the hills. Centuries of boots and wagon-ruts and the dragging weight of travellers too beaten to lift their feet. Every pothole catalogued itself as he walked. No gravel laid in decades. No scrub cut back from the verges. The neglect pulled at something behind his ribs, an itch that wanted *counting*, wanted to know which official had let this stretch rot and what pressure, applied to precisely which joint in the civic skeleton, would make them fix it. The hunger for structure curled warm beneath his skin, more familiar than the skin itself. Breathing was something the body did on its own. *This* was something he did.

Silence hung absolute over the scrub. No birdsong. No distant cart-rattle. Just the wet suck of his boots in the muck and the patient black attention of crows on a listing fence-post.

Whatever happened out here stayed between the participants and those crows.

*Perfect.*

## The Shape of a Lie

Survival, Banki had learned while still small enough for a crow to swallow, belonged to the creature capable of becoming unrecognizable.

He carried that lesson from the day the shining crusaders came to cleanse his birth-forest. They slaughtered the plainly dangerous fey first, moving with a righteous, terrifying efficiency, proving that good people were the most lethal creatures in existence. Once purity claimed a rigid definition, everything outside it became contamination. From the high canopy, the shape of their mercy had grown clear: strike the healer, blind the scout, break the leader, and wear whatever skin the world required to endure.

Now, he wore a human shape the way other men wore cloaks, with the absent ease of long practice. A man of middling height and unremarkable build trudged through the late afternoon chill, somewhere in his mid-thirties judging by the lines etched around his grey-green eyes. Those eyes carried the precise amount of weariness for a sellsword between engagements.

Beneath the flesh-weaving—beneath the bone and cartilage forced into mortal proportions—lay something ancient and jagged. Wings like stained glass stayed tightly folded against a spine sharp as a needle. A mind burned with a fire devoid of warmth. A pixie of the old deep woods, carrying in his hazelnut-sized skull an accumulation of tactical doctrine and raw, untamed magic that would have shattered a human archmage's sanity.

The world required none of that truth. The world required a lie walking a dirt road with nothing in his pockets worth stealing. Banki had always been extraordinarily generous with his lies.

He had walked for three hours when the emptiness of the road shattered.

He pushed his awareness outward, a silent cast of the mind slipping over the rolling hills. The ether rippled. Raw thoughts sparked in the gloom like candles lit in rapid sequence over the next rise.

One mind flared bright with pain and panic. Another ticked with sharp, self-satisfied control. A third remained cold, disciplined, holding itself utterly still.

A moment later, three more minds bloomed to the southeast. These were dimmer, moving fast through the winter brush, burdened by heavy effort.

Six minds in total. Three on the road, three in the scrub. The arrangement clicked into place with the precision of a tumblering lock. A broken wagon on the road ahead, a guard controlling the scene, and accomplices using the distraction to slip away with the cargo.

Banki's lips remained still. His stride held its rhythmic pace. His grey-green eyes swept the empty path ahead with the bored attention of a traveller possessed of entirely innocent intentions. Behind the mask, the ancient thing began calculating the grim arithmetic of the ambush.

## Cold Wind and Bad Intent

The sky above Greybridge hung heavy and bruised. Instead of stepping out onto the open road, Banki sank into the winter grass and scrub, letting his awareness stretch thin.

Fresh tracks bit deep into the brush, betraying multiple heavy-footed runners. A broken axle lay ahead, the wood shattered by a blunt strike rather than the wear of travel. A phantom restraint tightened and vanished under Banki's left shoulder, an old physical memory answering the threat of an orchestrated trap.

The wagon canted at a sick angle across the dirt, its left rear wheel snapped clean from the axle. The splintered ends shone pale and raw, the colour of exposed bone. Three figures stood around the wreckage in a crude morality play: the beaten driver, the authoritative guard, and the trapped traveller.

The guard wore a faded blue tabard bearing the Greybridge crest, the silver thread darkened to the shade of dirty dishwater.

Sixty feet separated Banki from the scene. He stood in the scrub brush, enveloped in a cloak woven with marrow-runes and bone-dust by long-dead craftsmen. The enchanted fabric drank the wind and the fading light, turning him into a shadow snagged on a thorn. The cost of wearing it gnawed at his core temperature, leaving frost in his veins, but the concealment was absolute.

He sank deeper into the minds ahead. The driver's panic spiked sharp as glass underfoot, a chaotic mess of agony and terror. The guard's thoughts ticked beside it in rigid clockwork, struggling to project outrage while managing the scene. The cloaked traveller remained a locked door with a pulse, trained enough to cage their fear, calculating the odds of survival.

Beyond the road, the three burdened minds thrashed through the brush. The carrier, the watcher, the fist.

Someone had built this stage by hand. They had positioned the wagon to block the road, cast the driver as a prop, and cornered the traveller as an unexpected complication. While the victims stared at the distraction, the real thieves vanished with the prize.

Safe and useless options presented themselves. Banki could simply walk away. But six minds offered six veins of leverage, and extracting the marrow from this scheme meant holding power in Greybridge before he even crossed its gates.

The choice crystallized. He would involve himself. He would take control.

The three thieves angled toward a bend in the road ahead. The guard's thoughts flickered toward the same bend, waiting for a signal.

Banki could reach the bend first. He melted into the brush.

## The Choir of Frightened Minds

Magic required no theatrical flourishes, no grand gestures to alert the prey. Banki adjusted his grip on his walking stick, weaving the spell into the mundane movement. His mental reach sharpened, cutting cleanly through the distance.

No one stirred. The guard maintained his bluster. The driver wept. The traveller stood frozen in disciplined terror. None of them sensed the invisible hand turning the pages of their minds.

Banki held his position, sifting the currents.

The guard's surface thoughts flowed rich and frantic: *...keep him talking... blame sticks if I keep the crowd framed... where are they... don't let the cloaked one bolt... Captain wants the goods quiet...*

Captain. A title carrying the weight of institutional command. The word "goods" echoed with bureaucratic neutrality—inventory, not plunder. The ambush operated on greased, systemic machinery.

The driver's mind offered only the pure, blinding noise of suffering: *...I told them I didn't have it—gods, my jaw—why would they hit me—just let me go—please don't take the wagon...*

Banki cataloged the driver's pain and set it aside. Collateral damage. No operational value.

The traveller's mind proved far more intricate: *...prints too fresh... three of them... don't look at the break—look at the guard's hands... if I move now, I'm next... where's my focus—stay calm...*

This one read the scene through brutal experience. The traveller recognized the fresh break and the wrongness of the guard's stance. A competent, disciplined survivor. Unshepherded, she might panic and ruin the board. Guided, she could become a crucial fulcrum.

Banki threaded a thought outward, piercing the distance like a needle passing through a cornea. The message had to be simple to survive the initial shock of mental intrusion.

*I am anchoring your mind. You can fight without restraint. The guard is stalling for partners and goods. Three sets of fresh prints in the brush. I will take the flank; you hold the center.*

The traveller's mind convulsed in a silent cascade of shock. Terror jagged upward, followed by a plunging chasm of confusion, before an ironclad discipline slammed into place. Banki read the jagged edges of her memories: she had been surprised before, and she knew the first second of hesitation brought death. Her hand twitched beneath her cloak and stilled. A breath caught, stopped, and returned under strict control.

*...not guard... help... okay... breathe... don't look for the source... just stand still and don't die...*

Banki allowed himself a flicker of professional appreciation.

He sent a final, brief directive. *When violence erupts at the bend, take the guard. Seize the narrative publicly. Make the driver your witness.*

A single, tight pulse of understanding echoed back. A compressed acknowledgment from a survivor ready to exploit a sudden advantage.

Banki withdrew the connection and slipped forward. He moved through the winter scrub like dark water through a cracked dam, slow and unyielding. Forty feet of brush stretched ahead. He timed his steps to the rhythmic sweeps of the guard's attention, stealing forward in the four-second intervals when the man's gaze swung toward the treeline.

Drop by drop, step by step, Banki closed the distance. A hundred and eighty pounds of lethal fey intelligence glided through the peripheral vision of the world, entirely unremarked.

The bend matched the image plucked from the guard's thoughts. A shallow cut in the terrain offered a natural blind spot. A low spine of rock, grey and furred with ancient lichen, provided waist-high cover. Between the rock and the thorns stretched twenty feet of open road—a kill lane. Anyone rounding the bend would stand completely exposed, unable to retreat without turning their back on the waiting executioner.

Banki settled behind the stone, pressing his illusory palms flat against the cold granite. He sifted through his vast repertoire of violence, selecting a working that others might consider excessive, but which he considered merely thorough.

His heartbeat slowed to a steady rhythm. The minds approached, heavy and confident.

Boots crunched on the packed dirt. A cold smile touched his lips.

## The Kill Lane

They rounded the bend carrying the swagger of men who owned the earth beneath their boots.

Three of them. The leader, built taut as a bowstring in patched leather, held a shortbow with the casual readiness of a lifelong killer. The second man loomed broad and thick. The third struggled behind, bearing the stolen weight.

Banki let them enter the lane. The kill required a cage.

He reached into the deep marrow of his magic, bypassing the slow chants of mortal sorcerers. He opened a vent to the deep earth, calling upon ancient roots and the void that slept beneath the bedrock. The magic drew heavily on his vitality, leaving a coppery taste in his mouth, but he welcomed the drain.

The ground erupted in a terrifying silence.

No explosive thunder warned the thieves. The magic manifested organically, the way rot blooms on a corpse or tree roots silently split a gravestone. Slick, writhing stalks of pure void burst from the packed dirt, reeking of ozone, stagnant water, and crushed bone. They were thick as a man's thigh, thrashing blindly through the dust.

The bowman met the void first. Three stalks coiled around his waist with the slow, crushing inevitability of a starved serpent. His shortbow clattered to the dirt. His jaw unhinged, but the unnatural pressure collapsed his thorax before his lungs could push out a scream. A wet, whistling wheeze escaped his lips, trapped and desperate. The stalks constricted further, and his ribs gave way with a sickening, wet crunch.

The carrier went down instantly. The stolen cargo spilled across the dirt as the shadowy vines seized his legs, dragging his face hard across the gravel.

The broad man proved tougher. Catching only a glancing blow to his shins, he stumbled but powered through with sheer mass. He staggered out of the thickest cluster of thrashing void-stalks, bloodied but upright, raising his cudgel to search for a target hidden behind the stone.

Banki rose from behind the lichen-covered rock. He gathered the ambient magic, twisting it into a curse of compressed force and bone-dust—a death-hex refined into a screaming bolt of pale light.

He aimed at the bowman, though the man was already dying, and released the hex.

The bolt crossed the lane faster than breath, striking the bowman's chest. The concussive impact shattered the remaining structure of the man's sternum, extinguishing his fading consciousness in an instant.

Down by the wagon, the traveller struck.

She timed her move flawlessly. Her voice rose, piercing the chaos with absolute, ringing certainty.

"He did this! The guard—he set this up!"

The words hit the scene like stones breaking a stagnant pond. The beaten driver flinched, his eyes wide as he stared at the guard, suddenly understanding the shape of his own ruin. The guard whipped his head toward the traveller, his mask of authority crumbling into raw, calculating panic.

Banki tasted the guard's spiraling thoughts: *...how did they—who told—control this, CONTROL this—Captain's going to—*

"Lies! You're with them!" the guard shouted, stepping backward. But the accusation carried no weight. The driver now looked at him with the terrifying clarity of a betrayed man.

The guard reached for his belt, his fingers frantic for a signal whistle.

Banki drew a second screaming hex. He leveled his hand at the broad thief, who had turned toward the sound of the shouting. The bolt of concussive force slammed into the large man's shoulder, tearing the armour at the seams and painting the cold air in red mist. The cudgel hit the ground. The broad man dropped to one knee, his aggression evaporating into a singular, crystalline realization of his own mortality.

The guard spun toward the bend, twelve feet away, running hard. His fingers closed on the whistle.

Banki shifted his aim. The third hex found the target that mattered.

## What the Guard Knew

The blast caught the guard perfectly between the shoulder blades.

He was caught mid-stride, his body entirely committed to forward momentum. The concussive impact struck with the wet crack of a thunderclap, violently accelerating the man's flight while destroying his ability to control it. The guard vaulted through the air, his limbs windmilling uselessly. The metal whistle slipped from his grasp, tumbling away into the deep scrub.

He hit the dirt face-first and skidded to a halt. Groaning, he dragged himself to his hands and knees, his mind a shrieking mess of denial and agony. *...magic—who—can't—Captain, Captain, CAPTAIN...*

Banki stepped clear of the rock.

He offered no grand pronouncements. The road simply yielded a man in a dark cloak, eyes cold as a frozen river, his right hand still smoking with the residue of a shattered hex.

"Stay down," Banki commanded, his tone dropping to a quiet, iron rasp.

The guard collapsed into the dirt.

Banki crossed the distance with unhurried, measured steps, coming to stand over the broken man.

"Captain Veller," he murmured.

The guard froze. Raw, animal terror eclipsed his pain.

*...how does he—no one knows—who is this—HOW DOES HE KNOW—*

"The south culvert," Banki continued, his voice perfectly level. "The Greybridge tollhouse ledger."

Each phrase hammered into the guard's fragile composure, detonating the walls protecting his secrets. As the defenses shattered, the information flooded into Banki's mind. He drank the torrent of memories: the names of the garrison conspirators, the smuggling routes, the ferry marks, the bribes, and the shallow graves hidden beneath freshly poured foundations. He archived every detail in the dark vaults of his mind, savoring the acquisition.

The guard sobbed uncontrollably into the dust. Banki offered the man no pity.

Four minutes later, the traveller walked up the bend.

The void-stalks had dissolved back into the earth, leaving only scored dirt and the broken bodies of the thieves. Banki had bound all three survivors with coarse rope.

The traveller stopped ten feet away and lowered her hood, revealing a woman with sharp, exhausted features.

She carried no obvious weapons, but Banki's arcane sight traced a faint thread of warding magic woven into her cloak, a quickening charm around her boots, and a vicious, single-use evocation trapped within a silver ring on her right hand. She stood perfectly balanced, evaluating him with the cold precision of a veteran who knew precisely how quickly men could turn into monsters.

*You anchored me,* she projected no words, but he read the shape of the thought forming in her mind as she spoke aloud. "You anchored me in my head. You provided an invisible safety net."

"Yes," Banki replied, declining to elaborate.

She studied the bound men, the cratered earth, and the lingering scent of ozone and blood.

"What are you?" she asked.

"A traveller." The lie slipped out flawlessly. "One who happened to possess the right skills at the necessary moment."

Her eyes narrowed in rapid calculation. She weighed his usefulness against his lethality. "My name is Sable. And I owe you a debt I did not ask to incur."

"Then we understand each other perfectly. Unsolicited debts form the foundation of ancient pacts and every truly useful relationship." Banki smiled, calculating the exact value of the interest he intended to collect.

Behind them, the bowman finally stopped breathing. Neither of them turned to look.

## The Captain's Shadow

Banki wasted no time on interrogation. Questions allowed a prisoner the luxury of resistance and choice. Instead, he placed a hand flat against the guard's chest, pushed past the surface panic, and broke the locks on the lower vaults of the man's memory.

The guard shattered without making a sound.

Something in the breaking—that breathless freefall of a consciousness losing its anchor—lit phantom iron across Banki's wrists and throat. *Cold bands biting bone-deep. A Thessalan chair. The sweet cremation-stench of fey-blood on iron while the extractor flayed everything above instinct to smooth, dead ash.* His fingers pressed harder against the guard's sternum. He had survived his own breaking by driving the crucial names and routes below conscious thought, encoding them into heartbeat and breath while the machine sterilized his higher mind. A shattered vault looked the same from either side.

The ledger spilled open. Drop points, cargo schedules, the fear of Captain Veller, the shadow of the patron commanding them all. Banki sifted through the wreckage, extracting every usable ounce of leverage, leaving the man hollowed out and weeping around his gag. The guard would remember the intrusion only as terrible, crushing weather inside his own skull.

Sable stood by, her posture undisturbed. The peripheral echoes of her memories—a brutal education on the streets of Blackwater Quay—marked this violation of the mind as simply another tool of survival.

"He will remember enough to be terrified," Sable said, her words barely carrying over the wind.

"Good."

"Terrified men run."

Banki released his grip on the guard, his grey-green eyes gleaming with the deep, cold light of the forest. "Let him run. A running man always leads you back to whoever holds his leash."
