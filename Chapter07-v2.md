# Chapter 7

## The Brain and the Binding

The Pink Annex derived its name from the pale, rose-hued crust of oxidation flaking across the heavy brass bulkheads—a chemical scar left by decades of continuous alchemical scrubbing. The isolation bay reeked of that sterile decay: a harsh, metallic tang laced with the biting sting of ozone and absolute containment. Every four seconds, the primary air-cyclers struck with a dull, hydraulic thud, dragging out the stale atmosphere and forcing in a fresh, hyper-oxygenated mixture.

Mirell stood beside Crate 12, frozen as stone. Her right palm hovered an inch above the manual kill-feed actuator. She wore a heavy Class-Four hazard rig, the vulcanized rubber and lead-weave mesh completely hiding her features behind a dark, polarized visor.

Crate 12 sat in the precise center of the reinforced flagstones. Its internal lead lattice was deliberately thick—a cage forged to smother the psychic radiation of whatever survived within. Thick cryo-rime frosted the edges of the steel, the violent thermal differential causing moisture to condense and pool around the base in a creeping white fog. The crate vibrated. Not with physical force, but with a dense, sub-audible psychic thrum that set the teeth aching and blurred vision at the edges.

A heavy lead-glass observation pane divided the command blister from the hot-zone. Up in the blister, the air was warm, smelling of brass polish and dry parchment. But physical distance meant nothing to the work at hand.

To slice a clean, intact skill-web from an Illithid-adjacent brain, physical proximity had to be absolute. The psychic bridge demanded a terminal anchor within three feet of the dying skull. Banki’s own physical frame carried too many conflicting arcane signatures; bringing his true body into the hot-zone would distort the delicate extraction geometry and shatter the prize.

Sable provided the vessel.

She stood opposite the crate from Mirell, clad in a matching hazard suit. Through the thick glass, Sable’s chest rose and fell in a slow, combat-steady cadence. She was ready. Operational consent had been established hours ago in the tactical briefing.

The incantation tasted of cold iron and stagnant water. The possession weave took hold.

The physical reality of the command blister dissolved, replaced by the violent, roaring transit of the astral tether. A microsecond of formless grey acceleration, followed by the brutal, bone-jarring impact into Sable’s nervous system.

The shock of entry rattled down her spine. Her pulse hammered against their shared awareness—ten beats faster than the resting rate left behind in the blister, a coiled spring of veteran adrenaline. Her muscle tissue thrummed with kinetic memory—the sharp, instinctual reflex of an operative auditing the room for threat vectors.

Sable offered zero fight. With terrifying military discipline, her consciousness folded itself back, stepping clear of the control levers and settling into the passenger seat of her own skull.

*<The floor is yours,>* her thought whispered—faint, relegated to the background, a ghost in the machine of her own flesh.

The hot-zone hit differently through Sable’s eyes and the polarized visor. The air was colder, biting through the thermal under-layers. The sterile, recycled oxygen from the rebreather tasted of copper wire and dry ice.

Banki pushed his psychic sweep straight through the crate’s physical seals. The invisible probe slid past the lead lattice and struck the brain inside.

The resonance bounced back—a jagged, terrifying echo.

It was no mindless beast. The scan did not merely map a biological mass; it mapped a predator that spent its life thinking about thinking. The consciousness generated a double-echo. It answered his scan, and a fraction of a millisecond later, it answered its own answer, analyzing the mechanics of his intrusion. A layered, sapient reflex. A fractal geometry of pure malice. It was Illithid-adjacent, and far too close to a peer conversation.

*Clean kill,* Null-Fang whispered in the dark of his soul. The artifact’s voice bypassed the possession tether entirely, thrumming straight into his core. Cold, precise, starving. *Loud truth. Zero second chances. The meat is rotting, but the secrets are fresh.*

Kestrel went rigid in the observation blister above. Through their secondary bond, her golden irises tracked the invisible currents of rising psychic tension. *<I hold overwatch,>* her thought brushed his mind—a heavy, steady anchor against the alien pressure. *<I will not interfere.>*

Through Sable’s hand, Banki gave Mirell a single, sharp gesture.

Mirell slammed her palm down on the actuator. The heavy solenoid clacked through the bay, followed by the violent, high-pressure hiss of aerosolized neurotoxin flooding the sealed crate.

Physical death required precisely six seconds. One standard combat cycle.

An agonizing, endless stretch in the psychic landscape.

Those six seconds belonged entirely to the skill-rip.

No crude, blunt-force trauma was used. That would reduce the neural data to useless static. Instead, an overwhelming, polite compulsion weaponized the bridge. A psychic architecture of absolute authority locked around the dying brain, commanding the consciousness to surrender its operational secrets with a structural logic the creature could not deny.

The alien mind realized its death at the exact moment its inner sanctum was breached. The double-echo collapsed into a telepathic scream that threatened to blow Sable’s ear-drums out from the inside.

Alien geometry and fragmented memories flooded the bridge. The structure of the creature's mind was non-euclidean—staircases of thought that folded back into themselves, entire libraries of stolen knowledge preserved in synaptic amber. Images of its hunts flashed across the tether—the cold, predatory logic of dropping onto a drow war-band, the bitter taste of terror as it unspooled the spinal cords of its prey, the suffocating, lightless pressure of the deep under-earth where it was birthed.

Its psychic defenses surged—a desperate, biological gamble against absolute subjugation. The entity attempted to construct an ontological paradox, flooding the receptors with the certainty that the extraction was not occurring, that the interrogator was merely a hallucination of a dying star. The sheer horror of the paradox clawed at his sanity, trying to rewrite reality from the inside out.

Banki increased the pressure. The statistical anomaly was crushed with cold, dictatorial force. The saving throw failed. The DC was insurmountable.

The operational baseline tore away. The lethal potential was stripped and cataloged—the synaptic pathways for cognitive splicing, the neural-lattice camouflage, the precise biometric triggers for mind-flaying. The data ripped out in pulsing, bloody chunks of raw thought, formatting the alien mechanics into ordered memory vaults.

Null-Fang circled the perimeter of the rip, devouring the psychic scraps, the terror, the agonizing realization of defeat.

The synapses collapsed. The mind shattered into ash. The double-echo flattened into a single, hollow tone, then plunged into absolute, permanent silence.

The threat ceased to exist.

The tether snapped back.

The violent recoil dragged his consciousness across the astral void in a fraction of a second. The physical re-entry was a car crash. The ambient temperature of the observation blister hit his true body like a hot wall. His heart, running much slower than Sable's, thudded sluggishly out of rhythm. The metallic taste of copper filled his mouth.

His heavy hand slammed against the observation glass, knuckles turning white. A hot, wet line of blood leaked from his left nostril, dripping onto the clean brass floor.

Down in the hot-zone, Sable dropped to one knee. She hit the flagstones hard, her gloved hands bracing against the frost-crusted steel of the crate. She shook her head violently, reclaiming control of her compromised nervous system, pulling deep, ragged gasps through her rebreather.

"Transferred and sanitized," Mirell’s voice rasped over the comm-link—a steady, grounded anchor in the ringing silence.

Banki wiped the blood with his sleeve. A heavy, metallic ache throbbed behind his eyes. "Log the resonance signature. Any future double-echoes are designated hostile on contact. Zero capture. Extreme prejudice."

## The Hierarchy of Stone

The facility demanded further order. The skill-rip provided raw data, but Sablehook required physical containment.

Down in the subterranean Registry Aux, a different problem waited.

The descent from the Pink Annex traded the sterile brass of the upper levels for the dark, reinforced spine of the complex. Polished stone walls swallowed all ambient noise, replacing the industrial hum of the air-cyclers with the crushing silence of the deep earth.

The door to the Controlled Pen stood at the end of an unlit corridor—a massive slab of black composite stone, thick enough to withstand a siege engine. Three independent verification matrices guarded the threshold, waiting like a silent tribunal that answered only to absolute truth. The platinum-weave sigils embedded in the archway alone had consumed three thousand gold pieces of refined residuum to etch—a security grid that guaranteed absolute isolation.

Stepping onto the threshold brought immediate physical resistance. The psionic pressure-field pushed back instantly—a heavy, invisible wall of force demanding a credential.

Banki threaded his resonance into the sigil-constellation. Not a push of brute force, but a perfectly machined key fitting a lock. He shaped his intent, projecting the core philosophy of his law: *Mine. Safe. Ordered.*

The divine-adjacent judgment weave audited the intent. It swept his soul for hesitation, for weakness, for the chaotic taint of the outside streets. Finding none, the system recognized the Authorized Anchor and stood down.

The heavy stone slab parted with the quiet sigh of expensive masonry.

The vault inside functioned as a fortified habitat. The air was cold, smelling of dry dust and ancient granite. A sheer wall of polished obsidian rose to the left, scored with deep, deliberate grip-slots leading to a high, shadowed ledge near the vaulted ceiling. Recessed light-fixtures cast minimal, angled illumination, turning the upper ledge into tactical high ground while leaving the floor exposed in deliberate vulnerability.

The Cliff-Skitter Predator hung halfway up the wall.

It did not pace. It did not crouch. It clung vertically to the granite—a nightmare of coiled muscle, chitinous blades, and prismatic, multi-faceted eyes. It was an assembly of intersecting razor-edges, designed to drop from the dark and sever a spinal column before the brain registered a shadow. Its carapace was composed of thousands of micro-laminar plates that vibrated at high frequency to shed kinetic impact. Its jagged outline distorted the air around it—a natural optical camouflage that bent light like heat rising off asphalt.

No greeting was offered. No submissive posture, no druidic attempt at kinship. The beast understood only one language: absolute control over the physical and mental space.

Banki projected his territorial authority into the room. He asserted the law of the vault, pushing his aura outward until it filled every corner. He set his shoulders, claiming the lower floor, making it explicitly clear that the flagstones belonged to the Anchor. Simultaneously, he ceded the high ground, dividing the room into precise, indisputable zones.

"That ledge is yours," Banki said aloud. The spoken words meant little to the beast, but the psychic projection beneath them carried the weight of an iron anchor. A shape of absolute intent—a blueprint of coexistence built on rigid, unyielding borders. "Perch there when you require distance. This vault is yours to hold. I am your anchor."

The beast stilled. Its multi-faceted eyes locked onto the floor, processing the psychic geometry. Those eyes did not merely read visible light; they mapped thermal signatures, arcane trails, and the microscopic electrical pulses of a beating heart.

The projection removed an uncertainty the predator was tired of carrying. The anxiety of unmapped territory vanished. A massive, scythe-like talon slid across the obsidian, the movement blurring the air into a shimmering streak. It shifted upward, dragging its heavy, armored bulk higher along the grip-slots, settling fully onto the recessed ledge.

It was not submissive. It was aligned. The predator recognized superior tactical positioning, accepted the boundary, and claimed its assigned zone.

The heavy stone door closed. The lock engaged with a deep, metallic thud, sealing the predator in its defined peace.

## The Tidepin Steps

The quiet of the lower corridor shattered against a spike of urgent telepathy.

*<Banki.>*

Sable’s mental voice was a razor-sharp needle driving straight through the hangover of the possession. Her mind was bruised from the skill-rip, but her operational clarity remained terrifyingly cold.

*<We caught a watcher. They breached the outer wards and slipped past Checkpoint One. They carried Darric's access token. They claim they were cleared for readiness mapping. The word used was ‘borrow.’>*

The ambient temperature of the hall seemed to drop five degrees.

*Borrow* implied an internal lane. It meant someone inside the network believed they possessed the authority to loan out access to Sablehook’s deepest vaults. Or, worse, an outsider was operating an insider who had drastically miscalculated the threshold for trespass.

*<Location?>* His mind snapped into cold, predatory focus.

*<Fleeing down the Tidepin Steps. Kestrel holds overwatch. I am dropping the lower bulkheads.>*

The Tidepin Steps. A brutal, spiraling staircase of sea-carved granite linking the deep vaults to the upper Mezzanine and the Reconciliation Room. The ancient access shaft was open to the coastal elements. The storm outside was at its crest; the Drowned Coast was famous for violent squalls that drove the sea upward against the cliffs.

The invisibility weave engaged.

The light-bending geometry slid over his skin like cold glass. The weave suppressed not just his visual form, but the sound of his boots on the flagstones, the rustle of his cloak, the very displacement of the air. A ghost slipping through the blast doors as they dropped, stepping out onto the precipice of the Tidepin Steps.

The roar of the subterranean tide hit him instantly. Freezing rain lashed through the iron grates, slicking the jagged stairs. Lightning flashed through the shaft, illuminating the spiral descent in blinding, strobing bursts of magnesium white. Every step was a risk—a wet, algae-encrusted ledge dropping away into the churning black abyss of the sea-cave below. Salt spray coated the granite in a layer of ice.

Frantic, disorganized boot-slaps echoed from below. The watcher was panicking, breathing in ragged gasps, slipping on the freezing stone.

Banki descended, tracking the intruder through the dark. The man was struggling with the brass-bound door of the Reconciliation Room, desperately jamming Darric’s forged token against the arcane reader. The magical friction of the false credential sputtered, spitting weak blue sparks into the gale.

Materializing from the invisibility weave directly behind the intruder required no blades. Steel was too intimate, too messy. Banki gathered his telekinetic authority, pulling the ambient momentum of the crashing waves and howling wind into a single, dense point of absolute pressure.

He drove the force forward.

The man's wrists and fingers were pinned flat against the freezing stone of the doorframe. The impact cracked against the granite like a broken spar.

No bone torque applied. No physical agony. Simply a new law of physics authored and enforced. The telekinetic field locked around the intruder’s joints. His physical resistance surged—a desperate biological effort against an overwhelming force. Banki increased the pressure, crushing the anomaly with absolute power. The DC was insurmountable.

*You may breathe,* the pressure dictated, *but you will not move.*

The intruder gasped, his lungs emptied by the impact. His fingers were splayed wide against the wet granite, completely locked in place.

"I'm not fighting!" the man choked out, his voice shaking above the ocean's roar. "I can't! I yield!"

Above, on the narrow, rain-slicked Mezzanine, the second watcher—the rear guard—tried to slip into the shadowed rafters.

Kestrel did not roar. She offered no warning. She simply ceased to be part of the ceiling and became an inevitable, violent outcome.

She dropped completely below the man’s perceptual threshold. To his nervous system, she did not exist until the moment of impact. Falling from the high beam-line, her massive bulk descended with the silence of a collapsing wall. She hit the flagstones directly behind his shoulder.

Her heavy forelimbs hooked inward with terrifying, calculated control. Her razor talons turned flat against his collarbones, refusing to tear the skin but promising instant dismemberment if he twitched. The grapple was won before his brain registered the threat. The opposed check was a mathematical certainty. She reclassified him instantly from an active infiltrator to secured property.

*<Target locked on the upper run,>* Kestrel transmitted, her mental voice completely steady despite the thrashing man in her grip. *<Holding for handoff. Perimeter secure.>*

The air in the Reconciliation Room smelled of wet wool, terror, and the ozone stench of the burnt token. The roar of the storm filled the silence as the telekinetic pin slowly erased the prisoner's resolve.

"Borrow." Banki's voice was flat, devoid of anger or curiosity. A cold fact laid on the table.

The man flinched, his cheek pressed hard against the frozen granite. "It was... a readiness trial! Darric said the outer lines were cycling! He said we had a ten-minute window to map the perimeter and pull back! Just an audit!"

"An audit requires an official ledger. It requires authorized sign-off. You are holding a forged token, standing at the threshold of a Level-Four containment vault, during a storm cycle. That is not an audit. That is an infiltration."

Banki pulled the token from the man’s locked fingers with a gesture. The brass disc was warm, thrumming with a forced arcane charge.

"Null-Fang," Banki sent inward.

*Let me taste the lie,* the blade rasped back, eager and vicious.

Banki pressed the flat of the token against his palm, opening a micro-channel to the artifact. No spell was cast; the sentient blade drank the residual psychic signature trapped in the metal. Null-Fang devoured the metadata in a fraction of a second, tearing through the deception.

*A cheap forgery,* Null-Fang reported, its mental tone dripping with aristocratic sneers. *Crafted three days ago in the Drowned Market. The signature belongs to a mid-tier artificer. Darric provided the base resonance, but the intent was purely mercenary. This rat was paid to map the vault, not audit it.*

The brass token dropped, clattering against the wet stone.

"You were paid to find the blind spots." Not a question. A statement of terminal fact.

The man closed his eyes, a thin whine escaping his throat. "Please... I just took the work. Five hundred gold to trace the ward-lines! That's all!"

"Who holds the contract?"

"I don't know! A proxy! A dead-drop in the Market! I swear on my eyes, I don't know the principal!"

The physical tells were absolute: the spiking pulse, the dilated pupils, the desperate pitch of his voice. He was telling the truth. A disposable asset—a blind probe thrown into the dark to see what bit back.

*Kill him,* Null-Fang urged—a slow, dark purr. *Drink his terror. Leave his corpse on the steps as a geopolitical statement.*

"No." Spoken aloud, addressing both the blade and the prisoner.

The telekinetic pin released. The sudden absence of pressure caused the man to collapse backward, hitting the wet flagstones in a gasping heap.

"You will return to your dead-drop," Banki said, staring down at him. "You will leave a message for your principal. Tell them Sablehook holds no blind spots. Tell them the wards do not cycle. And tell them that the next time they wish to borrow my shadows, the cost of the lease will be their head."

The messenger scrambled backward, his boots slipping on the frozen stone, before turning and sprinting back up the Tidepin Steps, completely abandoning his captured partner on the Mezzanine above.

A disposable mouth carrying a terrifying truth into the dark. The shadows in Sablehook were deepening, growing richer, heavier, and far more dangerous.

## Campfire in the Dark

The inner containment spine of the Black Labs thrummed with a vastly different energy than the upper levels. If the Pink Annex was a sterile hospital, the Black Labs were an abyss. The air was unnaturally heavy, saturated with the thick silence of a physical space that had been magically beaten into total submission.

The wardskins lining the masonry were not painted; they were tattooed directly into the granite using powdered cold-silver and basilisk blood. The containment runes thrummed with a slow, violet light, breathing in sync with the facility’s core.

Banki stretched his inner sight across the wardskins, mapping the boundaries of the absolute dark.

*About time,* Null-Fang sneered inside his skull. The artifact's presence slid between his ribs like a razor, applying deliberate pressure, demanding recognition. Null-Fang had been forged in an age when blood-magic was standard currency. It had drunk the marrow of forgotten kings and severed the tethers of minor gods. It understood no restraint. It knew only the hierarchy of violence, and until tonight, it had viewed its wielder as a convenient vessel to carry it to the next slaughter. *You keep summoning me like I’m a lantern. I am a mouth. I am a witness. I am a problem with manners. Ask me what I see. Don’t just swing me at the dark.*

"Null-Fang, stand fast." The acoustics of the Black wing seemed to lean in, the shadows pooling around his boots. "I require your assistance in this work. But we will establish the hierarchy of this room before we proceed."

Banki leveled a cold glare at the empty air, driving the full weight of his telepathic authority straight into the bond connecting his soul to the weapon. He forced the memory of the skill-rip into the blade—the raw horror of the Illithid-adjacent mind collapsing, the crushing weight of the ontological paradox. He fed a micro-dose of that terror into the artifact—a violent reminder of the apex predator holding the hilt.

"Kestrel is a sapient being," Banki’s mental voice vibrated with absolute law. "She is not an asset. She is not a tool. *She.* She is Family. We will grow together. You will afford her the exact respect you afford the hand that wields you. If you attempt to feed on her, if you attempt to belittle her resonance... I will seal you in a lead box at the bottom of the ocean and leave you to starve for a century. Do we have an understanding?"

For the first time since Banki had claimed the blade, Null-Fang did not posture. It threw up no wall of chaotic malice. It absorbed the dictatorial force of the threat, processed the unyielding boundary, and recalibrated.

*Acknowledged,* Null-Fang rasped. The sneering edge vanished, replaced by cold precision. *I name edges correctly, or I cut my own fingers. Kestrel. She. Family. Logged. The hierarchy holds.*

*<Heard. Understood,>* Kestrel echoed from her high perch above the blast door. Her disciplined focus brushed his mind—a warm, golden light against the cold steel of the blade. She didn't boast. She integrated the classification into her worldview.

With the blade brought to heel, the darkness of the Black Labs served as a private proving ground—a secure space to share their expanding power.

Banki produced a piece of alchemical chalk from his pouch. The scrape of chalk on polished stone was sharp in the silence. He drew three large, distinct circles on the floor, spaced ten feet apart.

He named them aloud, his voice echoing in the gloom: *"Family. Neutral. Enemy."*

He stepped into the center of the vault.

*<Watch. Observe the physics of the snare.>*

Reaching into his cold reservoir of arcane power, Banki did not merely cast a spell; he tore a localized rift in the material plane.

A writhing mass of black tentacles erupted from the flagstones, centered between the three circles. The spell—Evard's Black Tentacles—normally manifested as a mindless hazard that crushed friend and foe alike. But hours of brutal discipline had fused the raw magical formula with the structural control stolen from the Illithid-adjacent brain.

The shadow-limbs did not strike blindly. They obeyed the chalk.

Thick, void-born appendages, dripping with the cold of deep space, coiled gently around the *Family* circle, forming a protective wall. They ignored the *Neutral* circle entirely, lying inert on the stone. But above the *Enemy* mark, the tentacles lashed upward in a violent frenzy, crushing the empty air with force enough to shatter plate armor. The sound of the imploding vacuum was deafening.

*<I own the grip now. The spell is no longer a wild hazard. It is a targeted extraction. I can exclude your signature from the trigger geometry. You will never be caught in my snare. The field remains safe for you, even when I break the physics of the room.>*

Banki closed his fist, and the tentacles vanished, leaving only the scent of ozone and void.

Kestrel absorbed the tactical geometry. She saw magic not as wonder, but as an angle of attack—a variable to hold territory.

Banki raised his right hand, pointing two fingers at a lead containment block fifty feet across the vault. No vocal incantation. Pure focused intent firing an Eldritch Blast.

The violet energy snapped across the room, striking the lead block with a concussive shockwave that shoved the three-ton mass ten feet backward. The heavy lead scraped violently over the stone, stopping dead on a pre-drawn line.

The blast made no sound in transit. It left no visible trajectory in the air.

*<The blast is kinetic positioning. The Repelling invocation allows me to dictate the distance of an engagement. One clean hit shifts a line by ten feet. And because the energy is contained until impact, the vector does not point back to my position. I can strike from the invisibility weave, and the room will not know where the gun is hidden.>*

He tapped two fingers against his temple, his eyes burning with cold light.

*<Some minds are loud. Some are heavily shielded. But I can reach further now, catching intent earlier in their cognitive loop. And if they hide behind psionic walls, I can match the pressure to the specific thinker. No more unknown hands in the dark. Magic leaves tracks, Kestrel. Intent leaves tracks. I am learning to read the footprints faster than they can run.>*

Kestrel shifted on her perch, her golden irises tightening.

"Would you like to learn the spell to Fly?" Banki asked softly.

Her head tilted—a sharp, two-degree adjustment. Her crest feathers flattened in a rapid, involuntary slick against her skull. Her pupils dilated, her golden irises shrinking to thin burning rings around pools of black. One talon clamped onto the stone, scoring a fresh line in the granite. A single tremor ran through the flight-muscles of her folded wings—the predatory twitch of a body told the sky was available.

*<Yes.>* Then, a beat later, calculating the tactical value: *<Not for escape. For position. For joy. For better guarding.>*

*<How is it learned? And what are the rules?>*

"It is a Tri-Weave imprint," Banki explained. "It uses your own chassis as the anchor. You do not need to flap your wings to generate lift. You pull the ambient mana through your crest, and you command the air to become a staircase."

He projected the arcane schematics directly into her mind, layering the theory over her biological instincts. To a terrestrial hunter, flight was a profound cognitive leap. Her brain had to remap the Z-axis, recalibrating the inner ear to accept that height was no longer a barrier. But Kestrel’s neural plasticity—enhanced by constant telepathic contact—absorbed the mechanics instantly.

The rules were clear. Indoor discipline applied at all times. Zero uncommanded flight in warded vaults—the risk of hitting an anti-magic snare was fatal. But outside, in the open air, the sky belonged to her. Her kinetic envelope would expand, granting sixty feet of aerial maneuverability per tactical window, completely divorced from gravity.

Kestrel processed the imprint, driving the new invocation through her nervous system in controlled bursts.

She dropped from her perch, checking her descent a foot above the flagstones. She hovered. The sensation of gravity releasing its grip was a revelation. The ambient magic of the vault coiled beneath her feathers, replacing the downward drag of the earth with a frictionless cushion.

She did not use her wings; she willed the air to harden beneath her talons. She rotated on a fixed axis, stopped dead, and executed a slow climb. She visualized the heavy, enchanted mithral-weave war-skin the artificers were forging for her in the lower rings—calculating drag, weight distribution, and heat retention, applying those limits to her flight path as she moved with measured care.

The air warped around her talons, submitting fully to her command.

*<I will not be clumsy in the war-skin,>* she concluded, reversing her vector and alighting on her high perch with silent grace. The magical field vanished without a ripple.

The bond between them hardened, forged in the deep quiet of the Black Labs. Banki looked up at his scout, his protector, his Family. The power humming through their veins was no longer a wild storm; it was a calibrated engine. They were building an empire in Sablehook’s shadows, and they were finally learning how to rule the dark.
