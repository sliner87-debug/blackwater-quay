### SECTION 1: PREDATOR'S VERDICT

Public titles could be bought, envied, bargained for, or stolen in the night. Men traded them like copper coins, wearing their little badges of authority hoping the metal would somehow seep into their spines and make them brave. Banki despised that cheap currency. In Blackwater Quay, a title was merely a target painted on the chest. True authority required no announcement. It simply occupied space until everything else suffocated or submitted.

Kestrel’s true place remained reserved for the private hours. She had earned vastly more than the lazy language men used when they meant *valuable animal* and hoped the animal lacked the comprehension to gut them for the insult. 

Her controlled pen waited behind three distinct thresholds deep within Sablehook. Each doorway grew quieter than the last. The air cooled. The ambient noise of the House—the scrape of boots, the murmur of the ledgers, the clinking of the sorting floor—faded into a dense, expectant silence. This was private dominion. The architecture enforced respect.

The outer door boasted iron bands thick as a man’s wrist. The hinges sank deep into the stone, heavily greased, utterly silent. Inside, the environment stripped away the polite fictions of human habitation. A climbing wall of rough-hewn basalt dominated the left flank. A bare perch beam, thick as a ship’s mast, spanned the vaulted ceiling. Rails bolted into the masonry offered avenues of high-speed transit. It provided just enough room for a thinking predator to claim the high ground before anyone foolish enough to enter could offer the floor.

She waited on the upper beam.

*Cliff-Skitter Predator. Broodline Prime. Tri-Weave Soulblade Line.*

The House intake dossier had labelled her lean. In Kestrel, lean meant absolutely no ounce of flesh existed without a lethal purpose. She crouched in the shadows, a silhouette of hooked talons, double-jointed hind limbs, and a tail held in a questioning, fluid curve. The curve usually ended in blood, but right now, it merely ended in calculation.

Banki stepped to the center of the pen. The packed earth absorbed his footfalls. He stopped exactly at the edge of leaping distance. A fraction closer, and he became prey. A fraction further, he became an irrelevance. The geography of respect demanded perfect positioning.

He tapped the lower shelf beam built into the stone wall. Thick oak. Deeply anchored.

“This is yours, if you choose it,” he said. The acoustics of the stone chamber caught the words, stripping them of echo. “Home-base. Perch-zone. No one takes it from you without my permission. No one but family uses it.”

Her head tilted. The movement was a violent snap of articulation, predatory and precise. One foreclaw lifted from the upper beam. The talons flexed, testing the air, then settled back against the wood. She analyzed the angle, the distance, the implicit hierarchy of the offer.

Then, she descended.

It was not a drop. It was a controlled application of gravity. She crossed the vertical face of the wall in three silent, blurring paces, then leapt to the indicated perch. She crouched low, wrapping her tail around the thick oak, narrowing her posture. It was an acceptance far more eloquent than any human submission. It was a strategic alliance negotiated in muscle and silence.

Many men would have reached out to touch her then. Many men needed tactile confirmation of their ownership. Many men deserved the permanent, disfiguring scars that followed such arrogant impulses.

Banki kept his hands at his sides. True connection required no physical bridge. He opened his mind, dropping the shields that kept the noise of Sablehook at bay, and offered a single, crystalline question.

*You may choose a name.*

The telepathic channel opened. It did not use words. Words were blunt instruments, heavily compromised by the liars who invented them. He asked for a true name, a concept holding multiple truths simultaneously, vibrating with the unique resonance of her identity.

He applied the precise psychic pressure of his sovereign will—a terrifyingly polite telepathic weight. An offer carrying the absolute, inescapable force of law. A request wrapped in an authority that made refusal unthinkable, not through force, but through pure, inevitable logic. 

Her reply arrived as a layered, crushing impression. It hit his consciousness like a physical weight, assembling itself into sensory fragments that translated into human concepts.

A wing-shadow drifting silently over a blood-stained ledger.
Talon-script tearing parchment exactly where the ink feared to dry.
A veil that cut the throat while covering the eyes.
Height. The absolute advantage of the high ground.
Motion. The blur between the intention to kill and the corpse hitting the floor.
Witness. The cold, unblinking observation of a world that deserved to burn.
Family. The vicious, unbreakable circle drawn in the dirt, outside of which everyone was meat.

Banki absorbed the synthesis. The Tri-Weave pulsed behind his scars, braiding the concepts into a singular, undeniable identity. He spoke the translation aloud, his voice dropping into the resonant harmonic of command. He allowed her the space to refuse the translation, though he knew she would not.

“Kestrelveil.”

Her eyes flared bright in the dim room. The ambient shadows seemed to lean toward her, acknowledging the shift in reality. The name was no longer a label. It was an architecture. It was doctrine.

*Kestrelveil.*

The psychic bond snapped locked. The first anchor of the Triad-Mind Structure seated itself deep into the marrow of his psyche. She was not a follower. She was the apex projection of his will.

### SECTION 2: THE FORGING OF NULL-FANG

Midnight possessed a specific texture in the second sub-level of Sablehook. The air tasted of cold iron, stale oxygen, and the faint, coppery tang of latent magic waiting to be weaponized. The secure workshop permitted no vendors, tolerated no witnesses, and possessed no harmless reason to exist. It was a crucible built for atrocities masquerading as statecraft.

Banki sat alone in the center of the reinforced chamber. The completed, dormant metal of the sceptre body rested across his knees. It was heavy, cold, and utterly dead. That would change.

The threefold binding—the Tri-Weave—woke behind the dense scar tissue webbing his torso. War, veil, and hunger. The three pillars of his existence, surgically grafted into his soul by the Consortium years ago. The psychic channels flared to life, burning with the familiar, structural ache. It mimicked the agony of a stone bridge carrying an impossible load, groaning under the weight, refusing to collapse purely out of spite.

He needed an anchor. He needed fuel. Raw magic was insufficient for this working; it required trauma. It required the specific, crystallized hatred that only decades of suffering could refine.

A memory flashed, summoned deliberately from the locked vaults of Year Fourteen.

Cell Nine.

The sterile, blinding light of the surgical theater. Teren Voss’s immaculate, silver instruments laid out on the tray. The smell of antiseptic attempting to mask the overwhelming stench of opened flesh. The blood-soaked recovery room where he had lain paralyzed, feeling the psionic wires knitting into his nervous system, rewriting his biology. The helpless, suffocating rage of being treated as a component rather than a living being.

Banki did not push the memory away. He embraced it. He let the bitter, black residue wash through his system, channeling the absolute horror of Cell Nine directly into his hands.

He placed his fingers on the cold metal of the sceptre. He did not treat this as a spell with a neat, academic name. He treated it like forensic predation.

The Active Sensory Stack engaged.

*Detect Magic.* The ambient leylines of the room illuminated in his mind's eye.
*Detect Thoughts.* The residual psychic echoes of the House above filtered down, a chaotic hum he ruthlessly categorized and ignored.
*Read Minds.* His deep telepathic sensitivity expanded, seeking the intrinsic, dormant identity of the iron itself.
*Tri-Weave.* The resonance control locked the threads together, stabilizing the chaotic influx of power.

He pushed his intent into the metal. It was a psychometric working executed in reverse. Instead of reading the history of an object, he was writing a future into it.

The Tri-Weave flowed down his arms, a visible, crackling current of violet and black energy. It struck the sceptre, threading the dense iron with horrific marrow-runes. The metal hissed. The temperature in the room plummeted, frost blooming across the stone floor. The enchantment sank deep, violently transforming the material on a molecular level. It was a triumph of iron-bound sorcery, a violation of natural law.

The artifact fought back. The iron resisted the psychic intrusion, vibrating with a frequency that threatened to shatter his bones.

Banki dug deeper. He summoned the sovereign override, a localized, internal manifestation of absolute authority. A spike of pure, unadulterated will. The rules of reality bent, groaning under the pressure of a mind that refused to acknowledge limitations.

The sceptre cracked, then seamlessly re-fused. The metal shifted from a dull gray to a light-swallowing black. It stopped resisting. It surrendered.

The artifact woke.

It did not pulse with a steady, comforting magical light. It held a coiled, pressured purpose. It felt like a knot someone had tied in the fabric of the world and forgotten to cut.

“Null-Fang,” Banki whispered. The word tasted like ozone and ash.

He named it with absolute, cold awareness of the Divine Calculus. *Null* represented the void between systems, the frequency that defied all wards, the silence that consumed all arguments. *Fang* represented the application of that void. The physical penetration. The blood drawn from targets ripe for consumption.

Null-Fang hummed psionically. The vibration traveled up Banki’s arms, sliding directly into his nerve clusters, nesting perfectly into the scar tissue where the Tri-Weave lived. It locked into place with the precise satisfaction of a heavy tumbler turning in a vault door. The second anchor of the Triad-Mind Structure was secure.

Banki, Kestrelveil, Null-Fang. A closed loop of sovereign violence.

Null-Fang was not merely a weapon. It was an amplifier. An edge-keeper. It gave his telepathic strikes a physical weight and his physical strikes a telepathic rot.

He stood, holstering Null-Fang against his forearm. It replaced the obsolete, primitive eldritch focus rod he had once used to shape his concussive blasts. That rod was a tool for a warlock. Null-Fang was a tool for a sovereign. Patient, humming with dark continuity, the weapon waited for the inevitable application of its purpose.

### SECTION 3: THE RINSE BELOW

The Rinse Below did not exist on any official city registry. It operated in the murky, profitable space between high crime and high craft, buried deep in the sub-basement of a condemned Wharfside tenement.

The approach required patience. Banki navigated the urban decay with the precision of a scalpel. The fog rolling off the docks smelled of rot, brine, and desperate poverty. He bypassed three false approaches designed to funnel the unwary into ambush points. He descended two damp, crumbling stairwells where the stone wept black moisture. He stopped before a disguised door that looked like a collapsed load-bearing wall to the untrained eye.

The Crafters' Compass, glowing faintly in his palm, pointed directly at the masonry.

He engaged the cloak of invisibility. The magic washed over him, bending light and sound, erasing his physical presence from the material plane. He was a ghost carrying a void.

The breach was a masterclass in asymmetrical violence.

He stepped through the heavy door as it swung open to admit a runner. The runner never registered his presence. The primary guard, a hulking brute wearing patched chainmail and clutching a heavy crossbow, stood in the center of the antechamber.

Banki discarded drawn blades. He unleashed a concussive howl of arcane force.

The blast struck the guard’s consciousness like a smith's hammer against a cracked bell. The man’s eyes rolled back in his skull. The forge noise roaring from the inner chamber swallowed the clatter of the falling crossbow. The guard crumpled.

The assistant, a wiry youth carrying a tray of alchemical flux, turned at the sudden displacement of air. Banki stepped inside his guard, tapping the precise nerve cluster at the base of the neck. The assistant collapsed silently, the tray caught by a telekinetic cushion before it could hit the floor.

Banki positioned the unconscious bodies carefully against the damp wall. He refused to inflict collateral damage when minimal, precise effort prevented it. Unnecessary slaughter was the hallmark of an amateur.

He stepped into the inner workshop.

It was a miniature, horrific cathedral of glass, metal, and stolen lives. The heat was oppressive. The air smelled of ozone, melting sand, and the sharp, metallic tang of blood magic. Precision instruments lined the workbenches. Vats of bubbling alchemical solutions cast dancing, sickly green shadows across the vaulted ceiling.

Standing with his back to the door, the master craftsman—the Glasswright—worked.

For thirty-seven years, the elderly gnome had forged magical items in this subterranean hell. He manufactured amulets that projected false cosmetic auras to fool city watchmen. He forged weapons that boasted terrifying visual flares instead of lethal enchantments, sold to cowards wanting to look dangerous. He crafted flawless identity tokens that let murderers walk through the highborn districts unhindered. His ethics were entirely nonexistent, but his brilliance was undeniable.

“Don't touch the flux,” the Glasswright snapped, his voice a raspy, irritable bark. He refused to turn around, prioritizing his delicate, industrialized enchantments over basic situational awareness. “If the flux destabilizes, the whole batch goes up in a blue flame, and I will take the cost out of your hide.”

Banki dropped the invisibility.

The sudden shift in the room's atmospheric pressure made the gnome freeze. He turned slowly. His gaze caught the dark, unyielding silhouette of the sovereign of Sablehook.

Banki discarded interrogation. Interrogation allowed the subject the opportunity to lie.

He reached directly into the Glasswright’s mind.

The telepathic invasion was absolute. Banki tore past the gnome's surface defences — the brittle outer shell of practiced lies and professional secrecy — and drove straight into the deep memory vaults. He ripped the skill-web from the gnome's consciousness. Names, ledgers, client lists, blackmail material. He devoured thirty years of criminal enterprise in three agonising seconds, each stolen memory searing itself into the raw psychic tissue behind his eyes like a brand pressed into living flesh.

Two critical coordinates burned brightly in the stolen data: *Dry Kiln* and *Quiet Loft*. The next nodes in the hidden network.

Then, Banki tested a theory he had been cultivating for months.

Usually, the Sovereign Thread allowed him to consume a skill-web and graft it into his own mind, leaving the victim a drooling husk. Tonight, he sought a vessel. He needed the Glasswright's specific, highly technical skills preserved for future, directed use, isolated from the gnome's treacherous personality.

He found it resting on the secondary bench: a bone-fused gem artifact inscribed with intricate marrow-runes. A Twin-Gem Reliquary. The dual-pointed crystal had absorbed decades of ambient High Magic in this room, developing a resonant, internal lattice capable of containing a consciousness.

Banki stepped forward. He placed his left hand against the Glasswright's sweating temple. He picked up the crystal with his right.

He reversed the flow.

Instead of pulling the mind into himself, he pushed the gnome’s consciousness outward, forcing it down his arm and into the reliquary.

The gnome’s mind shrieked. The psychic scream threatened to shatter the glass vials lining the shelves. Banki clamped a telekinetic vice around the Glasswright’s physical vocal cords, choking off the biological sound. The silence in the room was absolute, save for the bubbling of the flux.

The crystal pulsed. The facets blazed with the luminous, violent exhale of a mineral lattice forcibly reorganizing itself around raw, terrified consciousness. One point of the gem glowed a warm, desperate amber. The other point shifted into a cold, clinical blue.

In the heart of the gem, a trapped awareness thrashed against the unbreakable glass walls.

The Glasswright’s physical body slumped to the floor. The open eyes adopted the specific, vacant, terrifying stare of an empty biological shell waiting for repurposed instructions. It was breathing meat. Nothing more.

Banki held the Twin-Gem Reliquary up to the sickly green light of the vats. The trapped consciousness pulsed frantically, beating against the crystal. It preserved every memory, every spell formula, every intricate crafting skill the Glasswright possessed, perfectly ready for interrogation and exploitation at Banki's leisure. A highly successful experiment. The Asmodean Pact in its purest, most efficient form.

He pocketed the reliquary, the warm crystal thrumming against his thigh. He secured the physical ledgers from the desk, sliding them into his coat.

He looked down at the empty shell on the floor. On the clean space of the workbench, he dragged a piece of parchment forward. He uncorked an inkwell and wrote a crisp, exact note, leaving it weighted beneath a spare iron crucible.

*NEW MANAGEMENT. REPORT TO SABLEHOOK FOR REASSIGNMENT. NONCOMPLIANCE IS INADVISABLE.*

Climbing back up the damp stairwells into the Wharfside fog, Banki didn't look back. The Rinse Below was broken. Its master lived in his pocket. He looked toward the sprawling, dark silhouette of the city, focusing on the remaining targets. Two more doors in the web still burned their lights.

He intended to extinguish them both before dawn.
