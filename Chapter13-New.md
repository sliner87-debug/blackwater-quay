The magic burned a toll from his stamina, leaving an ashen taste on his tongue, but the new male materialized with enough distinct identity to immediately strike the holding gate with a massive plated horn.

Darric dragged a hand down his face, releasing a rough, disbelieving laugh. "That is either the most expensive breeding pair in the world, or the most extravagant way to duplicate a problem."

Darric dragged his chalk across the slate, scratching out a hasty approval. Hesta grabbed the board, adding a stark warning beneath his handwriting regarding the vast difference between viability and civilization.

## The Military Line

Banki had initially dreamed of the Caravan Line—beasts painted with road-mud, carrying medicine through ambush country. Blackwater Quay had taught him a darker arithmetic. Corrupt guards and invaded cities required guardians with fangs.

He remembered Black-Yoke kneeling to let a rescued child touch his plated brow. A dock bell had rung, the girl had flinched, and Black-Yoke had pivoted toward the noise with sudden, devastating mass. The beast lacked a doctrine for punishing the thing that made a child afraid.

Sable leaned against the rail beside Banki, reading the tension in his shoulders. "You are about to turn a dream into a weapon."

From the gantry above, Kestrel lowered her head, her foreclaws biting into the wood. Her apex-predator assessment flowed through their bond: a caravan beast could save a road, but a war-beast could make the road afraid to become a cage.

Fourteen war bodies paced behind the reinforced glass. Captain Maela Thorn-Rusk evaluated the beasts with a soldier’s grim calculus. "You have a war-beast corps seed. If you field it prematurely, you create a massacre that happens to include your enemies."

"I am aware."

"You are informed," she corrected, tapping the glass. "Awareness arrives after you watch a beast cross a line you thought it understood."

Banki summoned the Tri-Weave again. The magic responded differently to the Military Strain, tasting of breached walls and absolute ruin. The conduit remained optional and separately activatable, bounded strictly to this agreement. He drew the infernal force by express recorded invocation, accepting the burden frame cleanly. 

Kestrel’s mind merged with his, unimpressed by the sheer size of the creatures. Null-Fang hovered at Banki's shoulder, the spectral weapon's haft-mouth tasting the ambient pressure.

*Bite the weak commands first,* Null-Fang murmured into his thoughts.

Kestrel countered instantly. *Make them harder to fool.*

The Sovereign Thread solidified into a heavy cable of Pact-magic. Banki drove the power through all fourteen beasts, measuring their survival instincts against his mandated adaptations. He pushed the working to a critical threshold, fracturing the arcane structure. He did not clone the beasts. Cloning invited stagnation. Instead, he forced a volatile biological triplication, splintering fourteen originals into forty-two distinct entities—each bearing slight, necessary variances to guarantee survival.

He channeled the blistering resonance of his pact, substituting delicate spellcraft with marrow-burning runes of command. *No later infernal interest in acts, effects, derivative forms, or strategic consequences arising from lawful use of transferred force shall be presumed or implied.* He hammered the legal stricture directly into their neural pathways. Asmodeus would claim no tithe on the blood these beasts spilled. The violence belonged exclusively to Sablehook. Any later infernal interest must arise only from separately recorded express terms, stated narrowly and without spillover beyond the exact grant.

The psychic backlash tasted like ash and copper, searing his nerves as he forced the discipline deep into their biology.

Hit. Halt. Wheel. Redirect. Capture without crush. Crush only when a siege left mercy no room to breathe. Kestrel’s overriding command anchored the chaotic minds. False voices dissolved into meaningless noise.

Banki pushed the heavy cable deeper. The handlers flooded the pens with drums, shouting, illithid domination grammar, and false pain. The young beasts held their ground, ignoring the noise. Only when the Crown kill-halt settled into their instincts did Sable stiffen beside him.

"You should have asked before binding me into the command structure," she said, her voice dropping.

"Lives are at immediate risk."

"That is an explanation, Banki. It is not permission."

Banki met her gaze. He held the chaotic, roaring magic at a standstill. The magic burned through his stamina, agonizing in its stasis, while forty-two enormous bodies trembled on the edge of receiving doctrine. He forced the working to wait until she made her choice.

Her anger shifted, cooling into something sharp and metallic. "Operational wartime command. Not ownership. Not a leash."

"Command in crisis. Refusal rights in misuse."

"Put that in the herd."

He wove her terms into the working. The second batch assimilated the magic perfectly. Rage cooled into tool-use. The beasts learned to navigate around allies, surrendered captives, and bonded handlers.

During the final drill, a handler dropped a command-token and froze. A young beast lunged. Hesta’s hand shot toward the stop-cord, but the beast simply scooped up the token and the handler, setting them both behind the safety rail before turning its massive horn toward the training dummy.

Darric slumped onto a crate, exhaling a long, shuddering breath.

Captain Thorn-Rusk peeled off a leather glove and rested two fingers against the glass. "Authenticated. Brutal, but authenticated."

Mara drew two distinct boxes on her slate. The first listed the Caravan Line, public and regulated. The second box carried Banki's and Sable's personal seals, marking the black-tier assets.

"If these war-beasts step onto public stone," Mara said, tapping the second box, "the city will remember the hoofprints. We will face songs, panic, and courts. I cannot let the law leash what it cannot survive seeing."

"Accepted," Banki agreed.

She snapped the slate shut. "You just handed me a future bill, Banki. I fully intend to collect."

Deep beneath the breakfast hall, forty-two massive creatures dreamed in displacement haze, twitching as they rehearsed the motions of war.

## First Contact

The Guttered Lantern served as neutral ground, its peace maintained by the massive, heavily armed proprietor, Darva Wellspring. Banki arrived early to meet Serren Vale, a broker handling the upcoming auction cycle. He chose the tavern precisely because its neutrality offered a shield against unwanted eyes. The air smelled of sour ale, wet wool, and the sharp brine of the harbor. 

Banki claimed a corner table, placing his back against the damp plaster wall. He kept the shrieking, coiled pressure of his pact-magic tight in his palm, ready to unleash a localized devastation if the meeting turned foul. He let his telepathic awareness ripple outward, touching the ambient minds in the tavern like a spider resting upon a wide web. The lower rumor channels hummed with alignment-drift. The city was actively deciding whether to attach itself to his Hall, keep its head down, or gamble on the old power webs.

The tavern door swung open. A man stepped through, blazing in Banki’s telepathic field like a bonfire in a cellar.

Aldric Thorne carried a heavy, imposing certainty. Though dressed in common wool and leather, he moved with the rigid bearing of a man accustomed to plate armor. His eyes swept the room, seeking burdens to shoulder rather than exits to exploit. A woman followed him—Maren. The bard carried no instrument today. She wore unassuming, functional clothes and a warm, strategic smile that invited conversation while betraying nothing. 

They claimed a table three paces away.

Banki’s telepathic sense brushed Aldric’s mind, immediately encountering a blinding wall of righteous purpose. Fragments of thought bled through the paladin's shields: memories of the Choir, a dozen restored minds, the divine authority of a sun god, and a lingering, unresolved shadow. Aldric was searching for the root of the city’s corruption, circling the edges of Banki’s own syndicate without yet knowing its name. 

Banki swallowed a spoonful of fish stew, carefully digesting the thoughts of a man who intended to burn his empire to the ground. 

Then Maren turned her head.

She locked eyes with Banki, possessing the unnerving focus of a predator who had spotted a mismatched shadow in the undergrowth. Banki arranged his features into an expression of mild, polite curiosity, offering the harmless smile of a businessman evaluating a stranger's attention.

Maren returned a brilliant, professional smile and stood, closing the distance between their tables with natural grace. "Excuse me," she murmured, resting her fingertips on the edge of his table. "I’m sorry to intrude. I’m new to the city, and you appear wonderfully established. Would you mind if I asked a few questions about the district? I’m struggling to get my bearings."

Through the telepathic web, Banki absorbed Aldric’s sudden spike of concern. The paladin's hand twitched toward a sword he wasn't wearing, a reflexive preparation for violence seamlessly woven into his unconscious habits. 

Banki broadened his harmless smile, burying his lethal readiness beneath layers of practiced charm. "Of course. Please, have a seat. I’m Banki. I run a modest salvage business down by the Wharfside. And the fish stew here is exceptional, if you haven't ordered yet."

Maren sat. She possessed a musician’s flawless timing, asking gentle, meandering questions that left wide silences for Banki to fill. Banki fed her true facts meticulously arranged to form elaborate deceptions. He applied the Improvement Doctrine to the conversation, treating every probe as a material negotiation statement. When she opened a scope for broad inquiry, he offered responsive refinements, narrowing the venue of the conversation until she found herself boxed into mundane logistics. 

He spoke of Sablehook, routine auctions, and difficult beasts. He offered nothing she could wield as a weapon, polishing every lie until it shone like absolute candor. He preserved the ambiguities in his own clauses, denying her any foothold to reclaim abandoned breadth. 

When she finally rose to leave, Aldric glanced toward the corner.

The paladin and the pact-mage locked eyes across three tables and a bowl of cooling stew. Aldric offered a small, exact nod of civility. Banki returned the gesture, mirroring the precise angle of respect. 

Maren glanced over her shoulder, catching the silent exchange, fully aware that both men were actively diminishing the weight of the moment. 

The heroes walked out into the damp street. Banki finished his stew, recognizing the exact shape of the looming war. 

## What Maren Heard

Banki remained at the corner table, wiping the last of the broth with a crust of dark bread. He did not follow them into the fog. Instead, he let his physical body relax into the chair while his consciousness snapped along the Sovereign Thread, expanding outward to blanket the street and the tavern’s exterior walls. Strict, suffocating control. He pushed his awareness into the shadow-fae construct perched on the dripping eaves above the alley, borrowing its faceted vision to watch Maren Goldstring. 

She did not go far. She slouched near the alley mouth, out of the direct lamplight, adjusting her lute strap to cast herself as a weary traveler rather than a spy. She maintained an expression of profound boredom. Directed interest carried a physical weight, and paranoid men always felt it settle upon them. 

Banki smiled, a cold, isolated expression hidden in the tavern’s gloom. She was good. But she was playing against a rigged board. 

Through the shadow-fae’s ears, Banki listened as two dock workers stepped into the alley to share a smoke. He had hand-picked them that morning. He had written the script, memorized the cadence of their natural speech, and fed them the exact phrases required to weaponize Maren’s own history against her. 

"...the salvage man possesses something massive underground," the older worker muttered, lighting a twist of cheap tobacco. "The new Sablehook foundations go three times deeper than any standard beast enclosure. My cousin poured the sub-level concrete. He swears the reinforced doors down there don't lead anywhere reasonable."

"Doors to what?" his companion asked, perfectly on cue. 

"He doesn't know. They dismissed the regular crew before the interior work commenced. A shadow crew took over—men with no guild marks, appearing at dawn and vanishing at dusk."

Banki watched Maren’s posture tighten. Her breathing shallowed out, tuning purely to the vibration of the man's voice. 

"It isn't the crew that terrified him," the older man whispered, the smoke curling around his face. "It's the noise. He went back after hours. Stood by the Sablehook fence in the dead of night. He heard something bleeding up from the deep earth. Not machinery. Something like... like a melody turned inside out. Notes flowing backward. He claimed the sound made his teeth throb in his skull."

"His teeth?"

"Teeth and bone. He suffered a blinding migraine for three days. The worst part? He doesn't remember walking home. One moment he's standing by the fence, listening to the inverted song, and the next he's waking up in his bed with three hours completely wiped from his mind."

A deep, glacial dread bloomed in Maren’s mind, radiating so intensely that Banki tasted it across the telepathic link. Memory loss. Inverted sonic harmonics. Subterranean vaults. He watched her construct the exact nightmare he had designed for her. She was connecting the false rumors to the Choir, the psychic threat Aldric had supposedly shattered. 

She believed someone was employing the exact same psychic tools—memory manipulation and sonic pressure—hidden beneath the noise of a salvage yard. She believed Banki, the salvage king with the unsettling grey-green eyes and the perfectly curated facility tour, was rebuilding the enemy's machine. 

A kitchen porter stepped out the tavern’s back door to dump a bucket of slops, shattering her quiet revelation. The young man, his sleeves dusted with flour, loudly defended the Sablehook yard to the older workers. He insisted the salvage king paid his wages on time and provided sleeping quarters with thick interior locks. He spoke with the fierce, defensive pride of a man desperate to believe his newfound safety was real. 

"An interior lock doesn't make a house clean," the older dock worker grunted, playing his part to perfection. 

"It makes it safe," the porter fired back. "It's the first lock I’ve ever owned." When the older man mocked him for selling his soul to the salvage king, the porter tapped a folded paper in his breast pocket. "The contract claims my labor, not my soul. I read it twice."

Banki absorbed the phrase, recognizing the perfect, accidental resonance with his own Asmodean negotiations. *Title vests fully and exclusively in Banki. No recall. No reversion.* The contract claimed the labor. It did not claim the soul. 

Maren’s fingers tightened around her lute case. Banki read her frustration, the sharp agony of moral complexity. A clean narrative of absolute evil was easy to confront. Instead, she faced a tyrant who commanded fierce gratitude from the broken people he employed. Any warning she offered the boy would sound like the cruelty of a privileged stranger trying to tear down his only shelter. 

Maren turned and walked out into the dense, brine-scented fog. The city tasted of deception. She refused to run. Running attracted the wrong kind of eyes. She paused beneath a flickering streetlamp, checked her back trail in a dark windowpane, and vanished into the mist to find Aldric. 

Banki withdrew his consciousness from the shadow-fae, the faceted vision collapsing back into the warm, smoky reality of the tavern. The heroes were hunting a ghost he had painted on the wall. They would waste weeks preparing for a psychic onslaught, completely blind to the forty-two armored war-beasts currently dreaming beneath the breakfast hall. 

He stood, tossed a silver coin onto the scarred table, and walked out to secure the rest of the city. 

## The Outer Carry Expansion

The creature alley smelled of ammonia, wet fur, ozone, and profound desperation. The market operated in the permanent gloom beneath the upper trade tiers, a chaotic sprawl of iron cages, glass terrariums, and heavy canvas tents. Handlers shouted over the shrieks of exotic stock. Brokers dealt in flesh, bone, and living tools. 

Banki moved through the crowd with Kestrel perched silently on a high rafter above, her telepathic overwatch feeding him a constant stream of threats and opportunities. He carried himself with the heavy, unhurried grace of a sovereign. He had come to build infrastructure. 

He paused before a wide, reinforced cage holding a low-slung, heavily muscled creature covered in pale, dust-colored fur. Silt. The warning beast. 

The handler tried to sell her as a brute courier, boasting of her jaw strength and stamina. Banki ignored the man entirely, sinking his awareness into the beast’s erratic movements. She fed on multiple kills, yet her value lay entirely elsewhere. Silt was not a dramatic alarm beast; she was a subtle early-warning specialist. 

Banki watched her pattern. A sudden whisker tension and directional flare indicated air and seam discomfort. A paw-scrape against the iron bars betrayed structural unease. She exhibited repeated repositioning, avoiding stale pockets of air. She remembered places that felt wrong, paths that closed badly, and shafts she disliked for physical reasons. 

She was a living tunnel diagnostic instrument. A smart handler could learn to read her before danger became obvious. She would test routes before he sent better stock or people, marking dangerous or air-poor passages. 

He bought her outright, transferring her immediately to a trusted Shadow for stable housing and documented signal work. 

He moved deeper into the market, stopping at a small, impeccably clean stall lined with glass jars, rendered fats, and mineral stocks. Widow Sarn stood behind the counter, a social selector and vulnerability mapper who had repurposed her sharp eyes for industry. 

"I want a contractor," Banki stated, bypassing the usual market haggling. He projected his practical authority, letting the heavy, Shadow-Tainted presence of his pact bleed into the air. "You will have a dedicated workshop area in Serik’s Yard, and you will also create for the Crown’s supply. We will pay for the infrastructure, but you will need to take at least three apprentices."

The surrounding alley heard only fragments, but Widow Sarn heard the whole future hit her in the chest. She did not react like a fool offered glory. She reacted like a worker suddenly shown the exact amount of labor required to deserve a miracle. 

She set both hands flat on her shelf and thought for one breath. Two at most. 

"I’ll do it," she said. No tremble. No bargaining first. 

"Conditions?" Banki asked, respecting her absolute professionalism. 

"I want clean jars. A real render station. Dry storage. Mineral stock not cut with rubbish. And if you want three apprentices, I choose which three don’t poison my work by breathing on it wrong." She shot a hard look toward the gambling dens at the edge of the lane. "And I don’t train gamblers."

"Accepted," Banki said. "I will add the workshop allocation, dry storage, render station, and apprentice intake to the yard refit plan tonight."

Widow Sarn looked at him deeply, taking in the cold efficiency of the exchange. "You do move fast."

"That’s one of the safer things about us," Banki replied. 

He turned away, his attention snapping toward a commotion deeper in the broker-shadow lanes. Chainmoss Vell, a notoriously slippery transporter, stood beside a heavy iron-bound wagon. His fake handlers milled around, trying to look busy, but Kestrel’s telepathic ping hit Banki’s mind like a physical blow. 

*Sapient cargo. Hidden in the crate beneath the stone-echo shrike.* 

Banki closed the distance in five long strides. The Shadows materialized from the crowd, moving with terrifying synchronization. Before Vell could shout an order, his assistants were pinned against the wagon, their weapons stripped. 

Banki stepped up to Chainmoss Vell, grabbing the man by the collar of his heavy coat and slamming him against the iron bars of the shrike’s cage. The massive bird shrieked, echoing the impact. 

The alley went sharply quiet. The fake handlers froze. The real handlers listened. The broker ears swiveled, memorizing the sudden violence. 

Banki turned just enough that the whole creature alley could hear the ruling. His voice carried like a blade dragged slowly across stone. 

"Hidden persons in creature trade geometry are not cargo," Banki declared, the Sovereign Thread flaring with absolute command. "Not now. Not later. Not under euphemism. Not under clever crate work."

He let the silence stretch, forcing every trader in the market to feel the weight of the new law. 

"If you hide a sapient in stock transport," Banki continued, his voice dropping to a lethal whisper that somehow reached the back of the alley, "I will take the person, take the stock, take the gear, and take the rest of your day apart until I know whether I’m looking at rescue, transport, leverage, or trafficking."

The threat landed perfectly. No one in the alley mistook it for rhetoric. The morally rotten began silently revising their methods or their routes. 

Chainmoss Vell tried to breathe around the iron grip on his throat and failed. 

"Take him," Banki ordered. 

The Shadows hauled Vell and his crew away. Another team cracked open the false bottom of the crate, pulling a terrified, underdark-born youth into the safety of Jeren’s protection. The stone-echo shrike, the wagon, and the gear were seized. 

Banki stood in the center of the sudden void, letting the market take its fresh read of him. He had contracted a serious supplier into infrastructure, and he had publicly defined and enforced a hard line on hidden sapients. 

He was not merely a buyer, a sovereign, or a creature eccentric. The alley smelled the sudden, terrifying mixture of profit, standards, danger, and opportunity that might actually survive contact with the law. He was a market-maker. A standards-setter. A man who would build a supply line and kill an entire category of illicit trade in the exact same hour. 

Kestrel’s telekinetic cradle adjusted the newly purchased stock without a wasted gesture. Widow Sarn was already mentally organizing bulk jars and apprentice discipline. The lower channels of the city were permanently altered. 

Banki adjusted his cuffs, his mind already calculating the next strike against the Velmaren trafficking ring, and walked out of the alley with the city firmly in his grip.
