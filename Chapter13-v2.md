# Chapter 13

The Sovereign Thread anchored without resistance, plunging deep into the wet, thrashing meat of the beast and yielding to the raw, crushing weight of Banki’s command.

He dragged his consciousness through suffocating layers of living matter—dense, fibrous muscle slick with lymph; the reeking, bruised hide; the shifting, arcane roots of the creature’s displacement glands; and the wet, coiling tension of its muscular tentacles. Beneath it all throbbed the erratic, terrified cadence of a monster’s heart. He forced his mind past the scarred psychic tissue where the creature’s ancestral instincts had learned that handlers were merely violent storms to be endured. The corrected pattern held. It possessed none of the elegant symmetry found in quiet, theoretical spell-craft, yet it remained rigid enough to channel the magic’s brutal, tearing current.

Viable. Distinct. A female founder, pinned to the stone and primed for the working.

Sable leaned over his shoulder, her hot breath whispering across the cold shell of his ear. "Do not turn her into a machine."

"I am forging a bloodline."

She shifted her stance, the oiled leather of her brigandine groaning in the damp gloom. "Men only reach for the word 'bloodline' when they want a machine that bleeds on command."

He ignored the sting of her words and drove the magic deeper, twisting the etheric weave until his own marrow throbbed with backlash. The second founder could not be a hollow duplicate. The working demanded a violent restructuring of bone density, ligament stress, temperament, and the ingrained primal terror that dictated whether a beast viewed an order as an act of trust or the latch of a cage.

The Tri-Weave flared into blinding, sickly prominence. A thick, suffocating cable of High Magic, dense with the tyrannical obligations of the Asmodean Pact, struck downward into the subterranean mist. The air turned foul, thick with the stench of corroded copper, charred parchment, and dried blood. Law, marrow, pattern.

A violent, sickening shudder tore through Banki's skull. The subterranean air shattered like brittle glass, replaced instantly by the blinding, chemical glare of a Consortium operating theater. He was flat on his back, pinned to a rusted steel slab by heavy leather straps that bit into raw skin. The cold, mechanical horror of live vivisection ripped open his chest as faceless chirurgeons peeled his flesh back to the ribcage, shoving crude, heavy arcanotech directly into his pulsing organs. Cold Iron surgical clamps bit deep into his Fey flesh, hissing, sizzling, and rising in greasy black smoke as they seared foreign machinery into his living biology. He thrashed against the iron, choking on his own bile, fighting a frantic, hopeless war for sovereignty over his own body while they carved him into an instrument of war. The phantom agony of the Cold Iron lingered—a white-hot brand scorched permanently into his nerve endings.

He ripped his mind back to the dark subterranean vault, gasping for air. *Ownership of transferred infernal force is now fully vested in Banki.* The legal clause reverberated through the stone, an unyielding metaphysical truth hammered directly into biology. *Infernal origin preserved strictly as classification.*

He slammed the magic through the corrected founder template, wrenching a male counterpart out of the ether and into wet, physical reality. The magic extorted a brutal toll from his reserves, leaving the taste of wet ash and iron on his tongue, but the new male materialized with enough raw, territorial violence to immediately smash its heavy, bone-plated horn against the iron holding gate.

The female founder lowered her own horn, her guttural hiss echoing off the wet masonry. The male backed down, his neck muscles quivering as he yielded to a dominance he understood in his bones before pride could earn him a fractured skull.

Darric wiped a smear of grime from his chin and let out a rough, disbelieving laugh. "That is either the most impossibly expensive breeding pair on this continent, or the most extravagant way to duplicate a catastrophe."

"I assume it is precisely both," Hesta muttered, her fingers tightening around the edge of the observation glass.

Mara’s regulations arrived on a slab of heavy slate before the ink on the first breeding registry had even dried.

*Founder-class living assets. Zero unsanctioned breeding. Zero sale. Zero outside loan. Founder protection prioritized over production yield. Dual-handler minimum at all times. Mandatory stress discipline. Zero proving-point exposure.* She shoved the slate across the stone table, her jaw locked tight, fully aware that a single ambiguous phrase in her text could unleash a bloody slaughter.

"Three-month gestation," Mirell reported, her flat voice cutting through the damp chill. "Projected yield: two to twelve offspring per female, assuming the reproductive corrections do not rot."

Silence gripped the chamber. A single beast was a localized nightmare. A predictable breeding yield was political power.

"Four pairs," Banki ordered, his voice rasping over a throat raw from infernal backlash.

Darric squeezed his eyes shut and cursed under his breath.

Hesta spat a foul obscenity onto the wet stone floor.

Sable rested her gloved hand on the rim of the slate, standing beside Banki without a breath of hesitation, refusing the quiet safety of a subordinate. Her mind remained psionically linked to his, ready to tear into the beasts’ brains if the glass shattered. "Why push for four?"

"Because a single pair is an anomaly," Banki said, watching the beasts steam in the cold air. "Four pairs form a species."

"Species become history," she warned.

"Yes. They do."

Creating the four line-true breeding pairs required a grueling marathon of etheric surgery, violent behavioral refusals, and Kestrel’s unyielding, raptor-like telepathic stares. They survived Mirell’s cold, anatomical dissections and Mara’s suffocating legal chains. By the time Direction Four held four stable founder pairs in its deep pens, a heavy, bone-deep exhaustion lay over the underground complex.

Banki exhaled slowly, letting the residual spellfire settle into a dull, throb behind his eyes, fully conscious of the razor-thin margin between a historic triumph and a catastrophic mistake.

## The Branch Strains

The specialized strains split naturally from the raw biological stock.

Branch A claimed the heavy road-guardians. The Caravan Line learned to carry staggering tonnage long before they were introduced to fear. A massive pair, Black-Yoke and Lantern-Rib, emerged as the undisputed anchors, kneeling placidly in the mud for harness fittings and rising without a tremor beneath massive loads of iron and stone. They kept their thick, coiled tentacles tucked neatly away from the yoke-lines even when handlers popped hidden ambush banners directly in their faces. They dragged weighted iron sleds through hair-pin turns, screaming crowds, and staged wheel-failures without spilling a single drop of blood or missing a stride.

During one drill, Black-Yoke braced a sliding, ten-ton supply wagon with a single, muscular tentacle, his hooves plowing deep furrows into the dirt. Lantern-Rib reached down and lifted a fallen, screaming handler by the collar of his oilskin coat, placing the man gently behind the timber safety rail with the terrifying, effortless delicate precision of a siege engine pouring wine.

Darric dragged his chalk across the slate with a sharp, approving stroke. Hesta snatched the slate from his hands, scrawling a dark warning beneath his signature regarding the profound gulf between physical obedience and actual civilization.

Branch C cultivated the discipline. The Refinement Line subjected older beasts to unpredictable handlers, deafening brass bells, simulated pain, and prolonged starvation. Soft-Chain Mother emerged as the matriarch of the line—a massive, scarred creature capable of checking three thrashing juveniles at a threshold simply by shifting her immense weight and striking the stone floor once with the hardened tip of a tentacle.

"That one," Kestrel’s voice projected into their minds, sharp as a striking beak.

The handlers froze, silent. The avian predator rarely deigned to use spoken words when telepathy was available.

"She teaches," Kestrel added, her golden eyes fixed on the beast.

Branch D kept the horrors. Everything that emerged wrong from the womb—twisted spines, ruptured venom glands, recursive pain loops, and broken command lattices—was funneled down into the dark holding vaults. Hesta insisted on giving every condemned creature a name before the surgical trays were unsealed. After the third name, Darric stopped trying to break the silence with jokes.

Mara never set foot in Branch D a second time. She simply went back to her desk and drafted tighter laws.

## The Military Line

Banki had originally envisioned the Caravan Line—great, docile beasts coated in road-dust, hauling medicine and grain through bandit-infested territories. But the blood and corruption of Blackwater Quay had forced a darker arithmetic upon him. Venal guards and predatory cartels did not yield to grain wagons. They yielded to fangs.

He recalled Black-Yoke kneeling in the mud while a rescued child pressed her tiny hand against his armored brow. A distant dock-bell had clanged, the child had flinched, and Black-Yoke had instantly swung his massive, horned head toward the noise with devastating speed. The beast possessed no doctrine for punishing the thing that caused a child terror. It only knew how to kill.

Sable leaned against the upper gantry rail, watching the shadows warp around his face. "You are about to transform a dream into an engine of slaughter."

Above them, Kestrel tilted her feathered head, her razor-sharp talons biting deep into the wooden beam. Her apex-predator instincts rippled down their psychic bond: a caravan beast could protect a road, but a war-beast could make the road too terrifying for cages to exist.

Fourteen heavy war-forms paced behind the reinforced glass of the low tier. Captain Maela Thorn-Rusk surveyed the monsters with the cold, unblinking assessment of a veteran mercenary. "You have the seed of a war-beast corps here. Field them a day too soon, and you will produce a massacre that merely happens to consume your enemies first."

"I am aware."

"You are informed," she corrected crisply, her gauntleted finger tapping the heavy glass. "Awareness only strikes you after you watch a beast cross a boundary you swore it understood."

Banki summoned the Tri-Weave once more. The magic answered differently to the Military Strain, rising thick with the stench of scorched gates, shattered shields, and unburied dead. The infernal conduit remained strictly optional, an isolated channel activated only by express, recorded invocation under the strict parameters of their agreement. He drew the infernal power into the room, accepting the crushing burden frame without a flicker of hesitation.

Kestrel’s mind locked into his, untroubled by the colossal physical mass of the creatures below. Beside him, Null-Fang hovered at his shoulder, the spectral weapon's haft-mouth slavering as it tasted the sudden drop in atmospheric pressure.

*Bite the weak commands first,* Null-Fang hissed into the dark of his mind.

Kestrel slashed through the spectral blade's thought instantly. *Make their minds impossible to break.*

The Sovereign Thread hardened into an unyielding cable of Pact-magic. Banki hammered the raw power into all fourteen war-beasts, testing their survival instincts against his mandated behavioral constraints. He drove the spellcraft to a terrifying crest, deliberately shattering the underlying arcane structure. He refused to clone them. Cloning brought genetic stagnation and fragile minds. Instead, he forced a violent biological triplication, fracturing the fourteen original bodies into forty-two distinct, living entities—each bearing subtle variations in armor plating and neurological routing to guarantee survival.

He channeled the white-hot resonance of his pact, replacing delicate somatic gestures with marrow-burning runes of absolute command seared directly into their bones. *No later infernal interest in acts, effects, derivative forms, or strategic consequences arising from lawful use of transferred force shall be presumed or implied.* He scorched the legal stricture into their neural pathways. Asmodeus would claim no tithe on the warm blood these beasts spilled. The violence belonged exclusively to Sablehook. Any future infernal interest must originate strictly from separately recorded express terms, narrowly defined without etheric spillover.

The psychic backlash tasted of hot iron and bitter ash, scorching his nerve endings as he forced the brutal discipline deep into their living tissue.

Strike. Halt. Wheel. Redirect. Disarm without crushing. Crush only when a siege leaves no room for breath. Kestrel’s cold, predatory dominance anchored their thrashing minds. False commands and illusionary voices dissolved into meaningless static.

Banki drove the infernal cable deeper. Handlers flooded the lower pens with deafening war-drums, harsh illithid domination phrases, and localized pain-glyphs. The young war-beasts stood like iron monuments, ignoring the chaos. Only when the Crown’s absolute kill-halt command settled into their brains did Sable stiffen at his side.

"You should have asked before weaving my mind into the command lattice," she said, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper.

"Lives were at immediate risk."

"That is a justification, Banki. It is not consent."

Banki turned his eyes to hers, holding the roaring, chaotic magic at a absolute standstill. The spellfire devoured his physical stamina, inflicting excruciating pain as forty-two massive, armored bodies quivered on the precipice of receiving doctrine. He forced the entire working to hover in agonizing stasis until she declared her choice.

Her eyes narrowed, the heat of her anger cooling into something icy and sharp as forged steel. "Operational wartime command. Zero ownership. Zero leash."

"Command in crisis," Banki agreed smoothly. "Absolute refusal rights in instances of misuse."

"Sear that into the herd."

He wove her exact terms into the living marrow of the beasts. The second batch absorbed the magic seamlessly. Raw rage dissolved into calculated utility. The monsters learned to maneuver around allies, drop their weapons before surrendering enemies, and protect bonded handlers with their own armored bodies.

During the final live-fire exercise, a green handler fumbled his command-token, dropping it into the mud, and froze in terror. A young war-beast charged. Hesta reached for the emergency kill-switch, but the beast merely scooped up the dropped token and the terrified handler with a single tentacle, tossing them safely behind the timber barrier before driving its massive horn through the heart of the iron training dummy.

Darric collapsed onto a wooden crate, letting out a long, ragged exhale.

Captain Thorn-Rusk pulled off her leather glove and pressed two fingers against the vibrating glass. "Authenticated. Horrifyingly brutal, but fully authenticated."

Mara drew two distinct boxes on her slate with deliberate force. The first box contained the Caravan Line—public, taxed, and heavily regulated. The second box carried Banki's and Sable's personal blood-seals, marking the black-tier assets.

"If these war-beasts ever step onto public cobblestones," Mara warned, tapping the dark box with her fingernail, "the city will never forget the sound of their hooves. We will face riots, public executions, and open war with the Magistracy. I cannot allow the law to defend what the public cannot survive looking at."

"Accepted," Banki said.

She snapped the slate shut with a sound like a pistol shot. "You just handed me a debt of catastrophic proportions, Banki. I intend to collect every copper."

Deep beneath the mess halls of the keep, forty-two massive war-forms slept in a haze of displaced ether, their heavy flanks heaving as they dreamed of slaughter.

## First Contact

The Guttered Lantern stood near the edge of the lower docks, a squat, timber-framed pit that smelled of sour ale, damp wool, rotting fish, and old violence. Its precarious peace was maintained entirely by its proprietor, Darva Wellspring—a woman built like a grain vault who kept a heavy iron cudgel beneath the bar. Banki arrived early to meet Serren Vale, a shady shadow-broker who controlled the upcoming livestock auction block. He had chosen the tavern precisely because its public neutral ground offered a shield against surprise incursions.

Banki took a corner booth, sliding his back against the cold, damp plaster wall. He kept the coiled, shrieking pressure of his pact-magic resting in his right palm beneath the table, ready to vaporize the room if the meeting soured. He cast his telepathic awareness outward, letting it drift across the minds in the tavern like a spider resting on a web of silk. The ambient thoughts of the drunks and dockers hummed with tension; the city was frantically deciding whether to align with his rising power, flee the district, or bet on the old noble families.

The heavy oak door swung inward, letting in a swirl of river fog. A man stepped across the threshold, blazing in Banki’s mind-sight like a torch thrust into a cellar.

Aldric Thorne moved with the heavy, unyielding certainty of a holy crusade. Though clad in simple wool and scuffed leather, he carried himself with the rigid bearing of a man who lived in full plate armor. His clear blue eyes swept the room, searching for burdens to lift rather than exits to hit. A woman stepped in behind him—Maren Goldstring. She carried no lute today. She wore practical dark clothes and a warm, disarming smile that invited confidence while revealing nothing of her own mind.

They took a table three paces away.

Banki’s telepathic sense brushed the paladin’s surface thoughts, instantly striking a wall of blinding, radiant conviction. Fragments of memory bled through Aldric’s mental shields: the golden light of the Choir, a dozen minds cleansed of corruption, the divine absolute of his sun-god, and a dark, lingering suspicion. Aldric was hunting the root of Blackwater Quay’s rot, slowly circling the edges of Banki’s syndicate without yet knowing its face.

Banki swallowed a spoonful of lukewarm fish stew, calmly digesting the thoughts of a man who intended to burn his empire to cinders.

Then Maren turned her head.

Her eyes locked onto Banki's with the terrifying, razor-sharp focus of an apex hunter spotting a shape that did not belong in the brush. Banki smoothed his expression into a mask of mild, harmless curiosity, offering the bland smile of a tired clerk watching strangers enter an inn.

Maren returned a radiant, perfectly calculated smile and stood up, closing the distance between their tables with fluid grace. "Forgive me," she said smoothly, resting two fingers on the edge of his scarred table. "I hate to interrupt your meal. My companion and I are new to the Wharfside, and you look like a man who knows these streets. Could I bother you for a bit of local direction? I feel entirely lost."

Through the psychic web, Banki registered Aldric’s sudden spike of protective anxiety. The paladin’s right hand twitched toward his hip—a muscle-memory gesture for a missing broadsword, seamlessly woven into his physical habits.

Banki widened his harmless smile, burying his lethal readiness deep beneath layers of easy charm. "Not a trouble at all. Please, sit. I am Banki. I run a modest salvage operation down on the quays. And the fish stew here is surprisingly good, if you haven't eaten."

Maren sat down. She possessed the impeccable pacing of a master performer, asking light, casual questions that left comfortable gaps for Banki to fill. Banki fed her meticulously crafted truths arranged to form elaborate lies. He applied the Improvement Doctrine to the dialogue, treating every quiet probe as a formal contractual negotiation. When she offered a broad inquiry, he countered with subtle refinements, narrowing the scope of the conversation until she was trapped discussing mundane harbor tariffs.

He spoke of Sablehook’s public salvage work, routine auctions, and stubborn draft beasts. He gave her nothing that could be turned into a weapon, polishing every falsehood until it gleamed like objective truth. He carefully preserved the ambiguities in his own statements, denying her any angle to reclaim lost ground.

When she finally rose to return to her companion, Aldric looked over at the corner booth.

The paladin and the pact-mage met each other's eyes across three timber tables and a bowl of cold stew. Aldric offered a stiff, precise nod of civic respect. Banki returned it instantly, matching the exact angle of his head.

Maren glanced back, catching the silent exchange, fully aware that both men were actively minimizing the immense weight of the encounter.

The two heroes walked out into the damp night air. Banki finished his stew, recognizing the exact, brutal shape of the war to come.

## What Maren Heard

Banki remained in the corner booth, scraping the last bit of cold broth with a crust of stale bread. He did not follow them out into the fog. Instead, he let his physical body slump into the chair while his mind snapped along the Sovereign Thread, shooting outward to blanket the tavern wall and the street beyond. Tyrannical control. He shoved his consciousness into the shadow-fae construct perched on the dripping stone eaves above the alley, borrowing its multi-faceted, obsidian vision to observe Maren Goldstring.

She had not gone far. She leaned against the alley wall just beyond the ring of lamplight, loosening her cloak to project the image of a weary traveler resting her feet. Her face was a mask of absolute boredom. Directed focus carried a physical weight, and paranoid targets always felt a spy's gaze on their necks.

Banki smiled—a cold, cruel twitch of his lips hidden in the shadows of the booth. She was brilliant. But she was playing a game on a board he had carved himself.

Through the shadow-fae’s acute hearing, Banki listened as two dockworkers stepped out into the alley to share a pipe. He had hand-selected both men that morning. He had written their lines, rehearsed their natural speech patterns, and paid them in silver to deliver the exact narrative required to turn Maren’s past against her.

"...the salvage boss has something colossal buried under the yard," the older worker muttered, tapping his pipe against his boot. "The new foundations at Sablehook go down three levels deeper than any beast stall needs to go. My cousin helped pour the deep stone. He swears those iron-reinforced doors down there don't lead to any cellar he’s ever seen."

"Doors to what?" his companion asked, executing his prompt perfectly.

"He doesn't know. They kicked the regular crew off the site before the locks were set. A shadow crew finished the job—men with no guild marks, showing up at dawn and vanishing before dark."

Banki watched Maren’s posture go rigid. Her breathing shallowing into absolute stillness, her ears straining to catch every vibration of the man's voice.

"It wasn't the crew that scared him half to death," the older man whispered, puffing a cloud of blue smoke into the damp air. "It was the noise. He went back after hours to fetch a forgotten trowel. Stood right by the Sablehook fence in the pitch dark. He heard something bleeding up through the dirt. Not machinery. Something like... like a song turned inside out. Notes running backward. He swore the sound made the teeth in his jaws ache."

"His teeth?"

"Teeth and skull. He had a screaming migraine for three days after. The worst part? He can't remember walking back to his lodgings. One minute he’s at the fence listening to that terrifying song, and the next he’s waking up in his own bed with three hours completely wiped clean from his head."

A cold bloom of primal terror erupted in Maren’s mind, radiating so violently that Banki tasted its metallic tang across the psychic link. Memory wipe. Inverted acoustic harmonics. Subterranean vaults. He watched her assemble the exact nightmare he had engineered for her. She was linking his false rumors directly to the Choir—the mind-shattering entity Aldric believed he had destroyed.

She now believed that someone was using the exact same psychic weaponry—memory destruction and sonic agony—beneath the cover of a dockside salvage yard. She believed Banki, the mild-mannered salvage king with the unsettling eyes and the polite demeanor, was reconstructing the Choir's engine of torture.

A tavern boy swung open the back door to dump a bucket of greasy slops, shattering her revelation. The boy, his aprons white with flour, loudly defended the Sablehook operation to the dockers. He insisted the salvage boss paid his men on time, in full coin, and provided bunkhouses with thick iron locks on the doors. He spoke with the fierce, aggressive loyalty of a desperate man who would kill to protect his new life.

"An iron lock doesn't make a house clean, boy," the older docker grunted, playing his part to perfection.

"It makes it mine," the boy snapped back, clutching his chest. "It's the first door I’ve ever had with a key." When the docker mocked him for selling his soul to a shadow, the boy pulled a folded parchment from his shirt. "The contract takes my labor from sunup to sundown. It doesn't touch my soul. I read the ink twice."

Banki absorbed the boy's words, savoring the accidental alignment with his own Asmodean negotiations. *Title vests fully and exclusively in Banki. Zero recall. Zero reversion.* The contract claimed the labor. It did not claim the soul.

Maren’s fingers dug into the wood of her lute case. Banki read her rising agony—the sickening weight of moral grayness. A simple narrative of monster and victim was easy to fight. Instead, she was looking at a lord of shadows who earned the fiercely defensive love of the broken people he employed. Any warning she tried to give the boy would sound like the arrogant cruelty of a wealthy outsider trying to strip him of his only shelter.

Maren turned on her heel and slipped into the heavy river fog. The city smelled of rot and lies. She refused to run; running drew the eyes of predators. She paused under a flickering gas lamp, verified her shadow in a dark shop window, and melted into the mist to find her paladin.

Banki pulled his mind back from the shadow-fae, the multi-faceted vision collapsing into the smoky warmth of the tavern. The heroes were hunting a phantom he had drawn on the wall for them. They would waste weeks fortifying their minds against a psychic assault, completely blind to the forty-two armored war-beasts sleeping beneath his mess hall.

He stood up, tossed a silver piece onto the table, and walked out to conquer the rest of the city.

## The Outer Carry Expansion

The creature alley smelled of raw ammonia, wet fur, ozone, and desperate fear. The market thrived in the permanent gloom beneath the upper trade arches—a screaming labyrinth of rusted iron cages, cracked glass terrariums, and reeking canvas tents. Handlers roared over the screeches of imported monsters. Brokers traded in flesh, marrow, and living weapons.

Banki strode down the center of the mud lane, Kestrel hovering silently on a high iron beam above him, her telepathic overwatch feeding him a constant tactical stream of targets and threats. He walked with the heavy, unhurried posture of an emperor inspecting a province. He had not come to browse. He had come to build a empire.

He stopped before a massive, reinforced cage holding a low-slung, heavily muscled beast covered in coarse, dust-colored fur. Silt. A warning beast.

The handler immediately launched into a loud sales pitch, hailing her as a thick-skulled courier capable of biting through iron. Banki ignored the man completely, sinking his consciousness straight into the monster's neural pathways. She had fed on human flesh, yes, but her true value had nothing to do with her jaws. Silt was a living diagnostic instrument.

Banki mapped her behavior. A sudden twitch of her whiskers and a flare of her nostrils indicated air contamination and shifting stone. A nervous scrape of her thick claws against the iron bars exposed structural instability beneath the floor. She constantly repositioned herself, shunning stale air pockets. She carried an instinctive memory of places that felt wrong, passages that promised cave-ins, and tunnels that lacked air flow.

She was a subterranean hazard reader. A skilled handler could read her body language long before a tunnel collapsed. She would walk the dark paths before he risked valuable stock or loyal men, marking dead air and weak stone.

He bought her instantly, transferring her into the hands of a trusted Shadow for immediate training and signal documentation.

He moved deeper into the market, stopping at a pristine, immaculate stall lined with blown-glass jars, rendered animal fats, and rare mineral powders. Widow Sarn stood behind the counter—a cold, sharp-eyed woman who had repurposed her anatomical genius for industrial alchemy.

"I require an alchemist," Banki said, stepping past the counter without a breath of small talk. He let the heavy, shadow-tainted authority of his pact bleed into the cold air. "You will be granted a dedicated workshop in Serik’s Yard, and you will manufacture exclusively for the Crown’s supply. We fund the infrastructure; you take three apprentices."

The surrounding market heard only vague murmurings, but Widow Sarn felt the full weight of his proposition strike her chest like a stone. She did not gasp like a fool offered gold. She reacted like an artisan shown the terrifying amount of labor required to forge a miracle.

She laid both hands flat on her wooden counter and calculated for two seconds.

"I accept," she said. No tremor in her voice. No haggling.

"Your conditions?" Banki asked, respecting her absolute competence.

"I demand clean glass jars. A proper rendering furnace. Dry stone storage. Mineral stock that hasn't been cut with chalk by dock-thieves. And if I am taking three apprentices, I choose the ones who won't ruin a batch by sweating into the crucible." She shot a venomous glare toward the gambling tents across the lane. "And I do not teach gamblers."

"Accepted," Banki said crisply. "I will add the workshop allocation, dry storage, render furnace, and apprentice selection to tonight's refit orders."

Widow Sarn searched his cold, grey-green eyes, taking in the absolute efficiency of the transaction. "You move with frightening speed, master."

"Speed is the only reason we are still breathing," Banki replied.

He turned away, his attention pulled toward a violent commotion deeper in the broker lanes. Chainmoss Vell, a notoriously filthy animal smuggler, stood beside a heavy, iron-bound cage wagon. His fake handlers were pretending to adjust the harness, but Kestrel’s telepathic ping struck Banki’s mind like a bolt of lightning.

*Sapient life. Hidden in the false bottom beneath the stone-echo shrike.*

Banki covered the distance in five long strides. His Shadows materialized out of the crowd like liquid ink, moving with terrifying synchronization. Before Vell could draw a blade, his assistants were pinned to the muddy cobblestones, their disarmed weapons clattering into the filth.

Banki stepped up to Chainmoss Vell, grabbed the smuggler by his greasy leather collar, and slammed him face-first against the heavy iron bars of the shrike’s cage. The massive, featherless bird shrieked in outrage, the sound rattling the iron bars.

The entire creature market went deathly silent. The fake handlers froze under the Shadows' blades. The real handlers stopped shouting. The brokers turned their ears, memorizing every detail of the sudden violence.

Banki turned just enough so his voice would carry to the farthest edges of the market. His words cut through the damp air like cold iron dragged across slate.

"Sapient beings hidden within creature trade shipments are not cargo," Banki proclaimed, the Sovereign Thread flaring with absolute, terrifying authority. "Not today. Not tomorrow. Not under any euphemism. Not under any clever crate work."

He let the heavy silence stretch, forcing every broker in the district to feel the crushing weight of the new law.

"If you attempt to conceal a sapient being in a stock shipment," Banki continued, his voice dropping to a lethal whisper that echoed off the high stone arches, "I will seize the person, seize the stock, seize your gear, and dismantle the rest of your life until I know whether I am looking at a rescue, a transport, a ransom, or trafficking."

The threat hit the crowd with brutal clarity. No one in the alley mistook it for legal posturing. The corrupt merchants began frantically calculating new routes around his district.

Chainmoss Vell tried to suck air into his crushed throat and failed.

"Take him," Banki ordered flatly.

The Shadows dragged Vell and his crew away into the dark. Another team smashed open the false bottom of the wagon, pulling a terrified, bleeding Underdark youth up into the gentle care of Jeren’s unit. The stone-echo shrike, the iron wagon, and the smuggler’s coin were declared forfeit to Sablehook.

Banki stood in the center of the quieted market, letting the district absorb its new reality. He had integrated a master alchemist into his supply chain, and he had publicly executed a lethal new law against human trafficking in the same breath.

He was no longer merely a buyer, a lord of shadows, or a monster trainer. The market tasted the potent, dangerous synthesis of massive profit, absolute standards, extreme violence, and structured opportunity that could survive the scrutiny of the law. He was a market-maker. He was the law.

Above him, Kestrel’s telekinetic field adjusted the newly purchased beasts without a wasted motion. In her stall, Widow Sarn was already cataloging glass jars and drafting apprentice discipline. The subterranean channels of the city had been rewritten forever.

Banki adjusted his cuffs, his mind already executing the next tactical strike against the Velmaren trafficking network, and stepped out of the market with the city locked firmly in his grip.
