The bruised twilight had finally surrendered to full, suffocating night. The city stretched out below them, a sprawling labyrinth of steep rooftops, winding alleys, and dark, churning water. 

Before tonight, the city had been a chaotic mess of competing factions, corrupt guilds, and desperate citizens huddled under the false light of the White Wax. Now, it was silent. 

The silence was not empty. It was pressurized. 

The Bell-Line surveillance, an intricate network of spies, informants, and shadow-operatives, was already adapting to the new reality. Under the direction of the Tri-Weave, they ceased to be mere gatherers of information. They became the immune response, hunting down the scattered remnants of the Covenant's loyalists, the debt-collectors, the enforcers who had thrived under the old, corrupt regime. 

Sable raised a hand, her long fingers splayed against the cold night air. The shadows in the alleys below shifted, elongating, snapping into sharp, terrifying focus. 

"They understand," she said, her voice barely a whisper, yet the sound carried the concussive weight of a falling guillotine. "The hierarchy is locked. The Quay is ours."

The Sovereign Mantle flared, acknowledging the truth of her statement. The absolute, undisputed rule was not just a political reality; it was a physical law of the city. 

But even an apex predator had to acknowledge the wider ecosystem. 

The Tri-Weave registered a faint, distant resonance. A pressure building against the outer edges of the Sovereign Contract, far beyond the physical borders of the city. It was not a threat. It was a gaze. 

Asmodeus. 

The Iron Adversary. The architect of the upcoming service year. He was watching. 

He was observing the foreclosure. He was analyzing the bio-mechanical breakdown, the integration of the WAR-LINE, the flawless execution of the Tri-Weave’s logic. He was measuring the exact dimensions of the sovereign champion he had bound by contract. 

The gaze was heavy with calculation, with ancient, unfathomable patience. It probed the newly fortified wards of Blackwater Quay, searching for a flaw, a backdoor, a hidden lever of control. 

It found none. 

The Sovereign Contract was ironclad. Blackwater Quay was not collateral. The city belonged to the throne. The Tri-Weave rejected the infernal probe with the same cold, mathematical finality it had used to crush the Covenant. 

A pulse of Hellfire, dark and thick with Krazak’s foundational lessons, surged through the Sovereign Braid. It was not an attack; it was a demonstration. The Hellfire wove itself seamlessly into the Tri-Weave, not consuming the logic, but reinforcing it. Fire, psionics, and living ward logic cooperating without corruption. 

The message was clear, transmitted without a single spoken word across the planar divide. 

*The city is secure. The throne is absolute. Send the trials.*

The distant pressure receded, leaving behind an impression of profound, terrifying amusement. The test of the service year would come. The Iron Boar, the Forge-Moon, the Maw-Gate—all the mythic horrors Hell could muster. 

But they would not touch Blackwater Quay. 

The Sovereign Mantle settled, the violet-black light within the Cerebrilith fluid dimming to a steady, operational hum. The WAR-LINE beasts, the Vanguard Branch, and the Widow-Tusk Crownsplitters held their positions in the dark, silent corridors of the Citadel, their tactical obedience unwavering. Kestrel remained perched on the blackened marble table, a silent sentinel over the paralyzed, breathing monuments of the destroyed High Council. 

Banki stood at the shattered window beside Sable, their shoulders mere inches apart. Neither leaned in. Neither offered a hand. The brutal, unspoken contract of their partnership required no such frailties. They looked out over their city, a terrifying, undisputed power-couple bound by shared darkness and unyielding pragmatism. The air still tasted of ozone and pulverized limestone, but the crushing weight of the foreclosure had lifted, replaced by the clean, undeniable clarity of absolute sovereignty.

The Covenant was dead. The lords were liquidated. The ecosystem was recalibrated. 

The Evening Court was permanently adjourned, and the harbor-crown belonged, forever, to the predators.
