Appendix C: Relics and Artifice of Blackwater Quay

The Quay is a place where high magic, psionic resonance, and desperate ingenuity collide. The items found here are rarely simple enchanted swords; they are complex fusions of Dhakaani metallurgy, Thessalan biomancy, and raw extraplanar energy harvested from the Void of Madness.

The following items are unique to the Blackwater Quay setting and should be treated as campaign-defining artifacts or highly coveted relics.

The Tri-Weave Silver Blade

Weapon (Longsword or Greatsword), Legendary (Requires Attunement)

Physical Description: Forged from an incredibly dense, ancient Dhakaani silver alloy, this blade is unnaturally heavy and perpetually cold to the touch. The fuller of the blade is etched with three distinct, overlapping runic matrices: glowing arcane sigils (blue), divine script (gold), and jagged psionic fractals (amethyst). When drawn, the blade emits a low, resonant hum that vibrates in the teeth of anyone within 10 feet.

History: Originally commissioned by the Dhakaani Empire to sever the psychic tethers of the Daelkyr, only a handful of these weapons were ever completed before the empire fell. Banki, utilizing his psionic intellect and Kael's arcane lattice, managed to re-forge a broken hilt found in the Sluices. It is designed specifically to break the Elder Node's control over the Quay.

Mechanics: * Enhancement: +3 enhancement bonus to attack and damage rolls. The weapon bypasses Damage Reduction as if it were Silver, Cold Iron, and Epic. * Tri-Weave Strike: The weapon channels three types of energy simultaneously. When you hit an Aberration, Outsider, or any creature currently under a mind-affecting effect (charm, domination, etc.), the blade deals an extra 3d6 force damage. * Sever the Thread (Su): Once per day, when you successfully strike a creature, you can trigger the blade's primary function. The target must make a DC 22 Will save. On a failure, any active enchantment, illusion, or psionic compulsion on the target is immediately subjected to a Greater Dispel Magic (Caster Level 20th). Furthermore, if the target is telepathically linked to another entity (such as the Elder Node or a hive mind), the link is violently severed. The target is Stunned for 1d4 rounds due to psychic feedback, and takes 10d6 points of psychic damage (half damage and no stun on a successful save).

Construction: * Requirements: Craft Magic Arms and Armor, dispel magic, mind blank, holy smite, creator must have at least 15 ranks in Knowledge (Psionics) and Knowledge (Arcana). * Cost: 85,000 gp, 6,800 XP, 170 days.

The Null-Fang

Wondrous Item, Very Rare (Requires Attunement)

Physical Description: A jagged, eight-inch obsidian fang extracted from the jaw of a deceased, high-tier Void-beast. It is wrapped in heavily tarnished silver wire and hung on a chain of cold iron. The fang seems to swallow ambient light, casting faint, inverse shadows against the wearer's chest.

History: Banki harvested this fang during his escape from the Thessalan Consortium's original breach ten years ago. It serves as a localized reality-anchor. The creature it belonged to fed on psionic energy, and its biology retains a localized vacuum that dampens telepathic frequencies.

Mechanics: * Psychic Void: While holding or wearing the Null-Fang, you gain Advantage (or a +4 resistance bonus) on all saving throws against enchantment and illusion spells, as well as any spell or power with the [Mind-Affecting] descriptor. * Zone of Silence (Su): As a standard action, you can activate the fang to project a 30-foot-radius aura of psionic and arcane silence for 1 minute (usable 3/day). Within this aura: * No sound can be created or pass through. * Telepathy fails to function entirely. * Creatures attempting to cast spells with verbal components, or manifest psionic powers, must succeed on a DC 25 Concentration check or the spell/power is lost. * Creatures with the Hive Mind trait lose its benefits while inside the aura.

Construction: * Requirements: Craft Wondrous Item, nondetection, zone of silence, must possess the fang of a CR 10+ Aberration. * Cost: 45,000 gp, 3,600 XP, 90 days.

Chronal Chronometer

Wondrous Item, Rare

Physical Description: A beautifully complex pocket watch constructed of polished brass, interlocking gears of fey-metal, and a crystal face that seems slightly blurred at the edges. Upon close inspection, the intricate hands occasionally tick backward, or stop entirely for a second before rushing to catch up.

History: Carried by Chief Navigator Serris Vae, this device is a masterpiece of chronomancy. It doesn't merely tell time; it measures the localized flow of the temporal stream, allowing the user to predict micro-fluctuations in reality.

Mechanics: * Temporal Prescience: The chronometer feeds micro-second glimpses of the immediate future to the wearer. While held or worn on the person, you gain a +4 insight bonus to Initiative rolls and a +2 insight bonus to Reflex saves. * Time Slip (Su): Once per day, you can twist the chronometer's master dial as an immediate action (or a reaction) when you are targeted by an attack or area-of-effect spell. You instantly slip out of the standard time stream, teleporting up to 15 feet to an unoccupied space you can see. This causes the triggering attack to automatically miss you, or allows you to completely evade an area effect if the 15-foot teleport takes you outside its radius.

Construction: * Requirements: Craft Wondrous Item, haste, blink, foresight. * Cost: 32,000 gp, 2,560 XP, 64 days.

Siphon Syringe

Wondrous Item, Uncommon

Physical Description: A heavy, intimidating medical instrument composed of reinforced alchemical glass, tarnished copper banding, and a wicked, hollow needle made of cold iron. The plunger is operated by a small, hand-cranked pneumatic gear system.

History: Developed by Suture in the laboratories of The Bleeding Needle, these syringes are issued to Sablehook's elite boarding parties (like Trench and Dreadjaw). They are used to safely extract mutagenic compounds, soul-ichor, and adrenaline from dying enemies to refuel the crew during extended sieges.

Mechanics: * Extraction: As a full-round action that provokes attacks of opportunity, you can plunge the syringe into a creature that has died within the last 1 minute. The syringe extracts their lingering life essence, instantly condensing it into a specialized, one-use potion based on the creature's original type. * Aberration: Potion of Telepathic Resistance (Grants a +4 bonus on Will saves against mind-affecting effects for 1 hour, and heals 2d8+5 HP). * Beast/Monstrosity: Potion of Bull's Strength (+4 Str for 10 minutes) and heals 2d8+5 HP. * Fey/Humanoid: Potion of Invisibility (lasts 3 minutes) and heals 2d8+5 HP. * Undead/Construct: Yields toxic sludge. If injected, deals 3d6 acid damage (no save). * Capacity & Spoilage: The syringe can hold only one dose at a time. Because the extracted essence is highly unstable outside a living host, the resulting potion spoils and becomes inert after 24 hours if it is not consumed or injected.

Construction: * Requirements: Craft Wondrous Item, Brew Potion, vampiric touch, gentle repose. * Cost: 4,000 gp, 320 XP, 8 days.

FateShear (The Severing Scythe)

Weapon (Scythe or Sickle), Legendary (Requires Attunement by an Evil character or a servant of the Daelkyr)

Physical Description: A massive, two-handed scythe. The haft is made of pale, petrified bone wrapped in flayed, tanned skin. The curved blade is not metal, but a shard of pure, crystallized void-matter—a localized tear in reality that looks like a window into a starless night. It makes no sound when it cuts through the air, completely absorbing acoustic vibration.

History: Wielded by the High Priests of the Choir of the Below, this weapon is a direct gift from the entities beyond the rift. It is said that FateShear does not cut flesh; it cuts the probabilistic timelines of the victim, severing them from their destined future and dragging them closer to the Void.

Mechanics: * Enhancement: +3 enhancement bonus to attack and damage rolls. The blade's edge exists partially in the Ethereal plane; its damage ignores all physical damage reduction (e.g., DR/Adamantine, DR/Magic) and halves all elemental resistances (but not immunities). * Fate's Rebuke (Su): The weapon strikes at a creature's destiny. When you roll a critical hit with this weapon, the target is subjected to a localized paradox. The target suffers a permanent -2 penalty to all saving throws and attack rolls. This penalty stacks with itself up to a maximum of -6. This is a curse effect and alters the target's timeline; it can only be removed by a Greater Restoration, Limited Wish, or Wish spell cast by a caster of at least 15th level. * Sever the Line (Sp): Once per week, you can attempt to cut a creature entirely out of the Prime Material Plane. On a successful melee attack, you can choose to deal no damage and instead cast Plane Shift on the target (Will DC 22 to resist). If they fail, they are instantly banished to the Void of Madness (or another highly hostile outer plane at the GM's discretion).

Construction: * Requirements: Craft Magic Arms and Armor, bestow curse, plane shift, enervation, creator must be Evil. * Cost: 120,000 gp, 9,600 XP, 240 days.

These feats are designed to reflect the unique magic, psionics, and biological modifications found in the Blackwater Quay conflict.

Blackwater Quay: Faction & Renown Mechanics

In the subterranean, magic-choked environment of Blackwater Quay, survival often depends on who you know—and who you owe. Players operate in a fragile ecosystem balanced between four major powers.

A player’s (or the party's collective) Renown Score with a faction starts at 0 and caps at 20. Earning Renown involves completing faction-specific quests, donating wealth or magical artifacts, or sabotaging rival factions. Note that earning Renown with one faction will almost always cost you Renown with their ideological enemies.

1. The Thessalan Consortium

  • Alignment: Lawful Evil
  • Focus: Biomancy, Controlled Mutation, Profit, Weaponized Evolution
  • Key NPC Contact: Overseer Varak (Head Fleshwright of the Foundry)

The Consortium views the Sluices as an unrestricted testing ground. They value ruthless efficiency, magical innovation, and biological perfection. They despise weakness, religious dogma, and anyone who stands in the way of "progress."

  • Earning Renown: Harvesting rare aberration cores intact (+1), turning over captured Templars for live experimentation (+2), assassinating rival smugglers disrupting supply lines (+1), mapping un-charted planar rifts (+1).
  • Losing Renown: Destroying their vats (-2), healing the sick for free (-1), destroying proprietary research (-3).

Consortium Rank Progression

  • Rank 1: Specimen (Renown 3)
    • Perk (The Flesh-Markets): You gain access to the Consortium's black market. You can purchase mutagens, alchemical weapons, and rare poisons at a 15% discount. You are no longer targeted by their feral test-subjects in the wild.
  • Rank 2: Stitch-Hand (Renown 10)
    • Perk (Bodily Perfection): You earn the right to sponsored modification. You can undergo one biological graft (up to 5,000 gp value) at no cost. Additionally, you can rest in Consortium safehouses, guaranteeing a safe Long Rest.

    • Perk (Master of the Vats): You command the foundry's output. Once per Act, you can requisition a Scour-Harrow or Dire Displacer Rhino to follow your orders unconditionally for one week.

  • Alignment: Lawful Good (Extremist)
  • Focus: Purity, Eradication of Arcane/Psionic Magic, Surface Order
  • Key NPC Contact: Inquisitor-General Vance

The surface-dwelling Templars view the Quay as a festering wound of heresy. They seek to purge the Quay of its illicit magic, destroy the Elder Node, and execute anyone bearing an aberrant mark or mutation.

  • Earning Renown: Turning in unregistered spellcasters or psions (+1), destroying black-market shipments (+2), providing actionable intel on the Tri-Weave Shroud (+3), burning mutant nests (+1).

  • Key NPC Contact: The Speaker in the Static (A Cerebrilith Avatar)

A terrifying cult of outcasts, mind-flayers, and mutants who worship the psychic static leaking from the Elder Node. They believe the whispers of the Void are the only true reality, and seek to tear down the Tri-Weave Shroud to let the "truth" flood the world.

Lore Deep-Dives: The Dungeon Master's Arcanum

This document contains the absolute truths behind the greatest mysteries of Blackwater Quay. Game Masters should read this to understand the deeper motivations of the factions, the underlying metaphysics of the setting, and the hidden mechanics driving the campaign's central conflicts. Do not share this document with players.

1. The True Fall of the Consortium (10 Years Ago)

The Public Lie: Ten years ago, the original leaders of the Thessalan Consortium pushed their biomantic experiments too far, resulting in a horrific plague that forced the Templars to quarantine the lower levels. The Consortium fractured into desperate splinter cells, and its original leaders were either killed or went into deep hiding.

The Catastrophic Truth: There was no plague. The Consortium discovered the Elder Node and attempted to harvest it, but that was merely the catalyst for a much larger catastrophe. The true fall was triggered by the violent escape of Bancaelis (now known as Banki) from his alchemical confinement.

In a brutal, desperate uprising, Bancaelis slaughtered every member of the Consortium cell holding him. He didn't just kill them; as a powerful fey, he tapped into the latent magical currents of the facility to rip them apart. The extreme magical frequencies and raw psionic pulses unleashed during this slaughter resonated with the Elder Node and the massive, interconnected experimental artifacts distributed across the Consortium's global magic web.

This resonance went catastrophically awry. It acted as a localized planar bomb, simultaneously ripping open Dimensional Tears to Xoriat (The Realm of Madness) in almost all of the Consortium's Cell Headquarters across the realm. Due to this sudden, extreme violence cascading across their interconnected network, 70% of the Thessalan Consortium's elite members were annihilated, driven instantly insane, or severely fleshwarped in a single night.

Sablehook was formed in the chaotic aftermath to build the Tri-Weave Shroud—not just as a localized jamming signal to hide from the Templars, but to act as a secondary, artificial seal over these cracked nodes and dimensional tears to prevent a full-scale planar invasion.

2. The Mechanics of the Tri-Weave Shroud

The Tri-Weave Shroud is the central technological and magical marvel of Blackwater Quay. GMs must understand how it works, as the players will be tasked with repairing, defending, and eventually upgrading it.

The Three Weaves: The Shroud requires three distinct, mutually hostile forms of magic to remain perfectly balanced. If one weave overpowers the others, the Shroud collapses. 1. The Arcane Weave (The Canvas): Powered by standard spelljamming cores and Ley-line taps. This forms the physical dome of energy that covers the Quay. It is stable, predictable, but fragile to brute force. 2. The Divine Weave (The Anchor): Sourced from stolen, corrupted Templar relics. The divine weave tethers the Arcane Canvas to the material plane, preventing the raw magic from dispersing into the ether. It provides durability. 3. The Psionic Weave (The Scrambler): The most dangerous component. Extracted directly from aberrant tissue and captive illithids. This weave acts as white noise, jamming the telepathic frequencies of the Elder Node and hiding the Quay from both Xoriat and the surface diviners.

The Flaw in the Design: The Tri-Weave Shroud is inherently unstable. Banki designed it in a state of sheer panic following the Consortium's collapse. It requires constant tuning and sacrifices. The ambient radiation from the Shroud is what causes the slow, creeping mutations seen in the citizens of the Sluices (often mistaken for "The Ash Vein Plague").

3. The Draconic Prophecy (The Quay Clauses)

The Draconic Prophecy is a sprawling, incomprehensible tapestry of events that dictate the fate of the world, written in the movement of tectonic plates, the alignment of moons, and the birthmarks of kings. Two specific clauses directly relate to Blackwater Quay and the players' actions.

Clause 44-Omicron: The Shadow-Stitcher

"When the stained-glass wings shield the sunken harbor from the eyes of the sun, the seal shall hold. But should the shadow-stitcher bleed upon the crystal, the door to the howling dark shall swing wide."

GM Translation: Banki (whose true fey form possesses stained-glass wings) is the architect of the Shroud. "Shadow-stitcher" is his true fey title. If Banki's blood is spilled directly onto the Elder Node, the Tri-Weave Shroud will instantly invert. Instead of sealing the dimensional tear, it will act as an amplifier, shattering the Node completely and opening a permanent, continent-sized portal to Xoriat. The villains in Act III (the Githyanki and the Covenant) are actively trying to capture Banki to bleed him on the Node for exactly this reason.

Clause 89-Sigma: The Child of Silver and Ash

"The child of silver and ash must choose: burn the rot to save the surface, or embrace the rot to save the damned."

GM Translation: This refers to Kael/Mikhailis. He has Templar heritage (silver) but was raised in the Quay (ash). The climax of the campaign requires the players (led by or influencing Kael) to make a massive moral choice: Let the Templars destroy the Quay (saving the surface world from the Node's eventual breach but committing genocide) OR fight the Templars to protect the innocents of the Quay (dooming the surface to a slow, inevitable psychic bleed over the next century).

4. Ecology of the Void of Madness (Xoriat)

The realm on the other side of the Elder Node is not simply "evil," like the Nine Hells or the Abyss. It is a dimension of non-Euclidean geometry, hyper-density, and raw thought. GMs should describe Xoriat and its denizens as profoundly wrong to the senses.

  • The Physics of Thought: In the Void, thought dictates physical reality. If you believe you are burning, you will turn to ash. The entities that live there (Aberrations, Cerebriliths, Mind Flayers) are not truly biological; they are coagulated, malicious ideas given terrifying physical form.
  • The Bleed Effect: The "psychic static" leaking into Blackwater Quay is simply the physics of the Void trying to overwrite the physics of the material plane. This is why biomancy works so well in the Quay—flesh is literally more malleable when the fundamental laws of reality are softening.
  • The Mutations: The mutations seen in the Choir of the Below are not diseases. They are physical adaptations attempting to conform to a new set of physical laws. A man growing gills in a dry tunnel isn't sick; his body is preparing for a reality where the tunnel is flooded with amniotic fluid.

5. The True Nature of the Elder Node

The Secret: The Elder Node is not a portal, a machine, or a crystal. It is a Dormant God-Brain.

Thousands of years ago, a nascent Great Old One attempted to enter the material plane. A coalition of ancient dragons and prehistoric fey managed to slay its physical body, but its brain—a massive, petrified mass of psychic tissue—crashed deep into the earth.

The city of Blackwater Quay is unknowingly built inside the hollowed-out skull of this dead god. The "Elder Node" is merely a single exposed synapse of the brain. The Githyanki are not here to close a portal; they are here to harvest the synapse to fuel their war against the Illithid empire. The Choir of the Below worships the ambient hum of the brain, mistaking its dying dreams for divine prophecy.

6. Secrets of the NPCs

  • Banki (Bancaelis): He is not a rogue fey. He was once an Archfey of the Winter Court, exiled for attempting to stitch the Feywild and the Shadowfell together. His mechanical eye is actually a phylactery containing the soul of his murdered brother.
  • Suture (The Rift-Sealer): Suture is not entirely alive. He died five years ago during a dive into the Deepmind Annex. His current body is an advanced flesh-golem piloted by his own ghost, bound to the rotting meat by adamantine staples. This is why he smells of formaldehyde and requires constant "repairs" rather than healing magic.
  • Zaniph (The Deserter): Zaniph actually works directly for Vlaakith. Her "desertion" was a deeply embedded long-con to infiltrate Sablehook, gain Kael's trust, and locate Banki. She is the mole that signals the Githyanki fleet in Act III. If the players befriend her early on, her betrayal should be devastating.
